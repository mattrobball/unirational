                                       Introduction
                                               The quantum cohomology algebra of a projective manifold X is the cohomo-
                                       logy of X endowed with a different algebra structure, which takes into account the
                                       geometry of rational curves in X . This structure has been first defined heuristically

                                       by the mathematical physicists [V,W]; a rigorous construction (and proof of the
                                       associativity, which is highly non trivial) has been achieved recently by Ruan and
                                       Tian [R-T].
                                               When computed e.g. for surfaces, the quantum cohomology looks rather com-
                                       plicated [C-M]. The aim of this note is to show that the situation improves consi-
                                       derably when the dimension becomes high with respect to the degree. Our main
                                       result is:

Example .− Let us go back to our favorite example, the cubic threefold, taking for
Y a generic line in X . Then Γ is a smooth curve; the map ϕ : H3 (X, Z) → H1 (Γ, Z)
gives rise to a morphism Φ : JX → JΓ , where JΓ is the Jacobian of Γ and JX the
intermediate Jacobian of X (see e.g. [C-G]); the formula (ϕ(α) | ϕ(β)) = −2 (α | β)
for α, β ∈ H3 (X, Z) given by Proposition 5 means that the principal polarization
of JΓ induces twice the principal polarization of JX . One deduces easily from this
that the intermediate Jacobian JX is isomorphic (as a principally polarized Abelian
variety) to the Prym variety associated to Γ and the natural involution of Γ which
maps a line L to the third line cut down on X by the 2-plane spanned by Y and
L – a fundamental fact for the geometry of the cubic threefold, due to Mumford
(see Appendix C of [C-G]).
