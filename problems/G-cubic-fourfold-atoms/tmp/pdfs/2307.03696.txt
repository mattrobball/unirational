                                                QUANTUM COHOMOLOGY OF PROJECTIVE BUNDLES

                                                                      HIROSHI IRITANI AND YUKI KOTO

                                                  Abstract. We construct an I-function of the projective bundle P(V ) associated
                                                  with a not necessarily split vector bundle V → B as a Fourier transform of the
                                                  S 1 -equivariant J-function of the total space of V and show that it lies on the
                                                  Givental Lagrangian cone of P(V ). Using this result, we show that the quantum
                                                  cohomology D-module of P(V ) splits into a direct sum of the quantum cohomology

                                                  D-modules of the base space B. This has applications to the semisimplicity of big
                                                  quantum cohomology.



                                                                               1. Introduction
                                              For a rank-r vector bundle V → B over a smooth projective variety B, we can
                                           construct the projective bundle π : P(V ) → B consisting of lines in the fibres of
                                           V → B. The ordinary cohomology group H ∗ (P(V )) of P(V ) is generated by the
                                           relative hyperplane class p := c1 (OP(V ) (1)) as an H ∗ (B)-algebra as follows:
                                                          H ∗ (P(V )) ∼
                                                                      = H ∗ (B)[p]/(pr + c1 (V )pr−1 + · · · + cr (V )).
                                           This isomorphism is a consequence of the Leray-Hirsch theorem that says that
                                           H ∗ (P(V )) is a free H ∗ (B)-module generated by 1, p, . . . , pr−1 .
                                              Many researchers have pursued a quantum cohomology counterpart to the Leray-
                                           Hirsch theorem for projective bundles or even broader fibre bundles, see e.g. [50, 10,
                                           1, 13, 41, 3, 52, 40, 39, 20, 14, 16, 15]. If we count only ‘vertical’ curves contracted
                                           by the projection P(V ) → B, we can easily observe that the corresponding ‘small
                                           vertical’ quantum cohomology of P(V ) is given by
                                                               H ∗ (B)[p, q]/(pr + c1 (V )pr−1 + · · · + cr (V ) − q)
                                           where q is the Novikov variable associated with vertical lines. For q ̸= 0, this ring
                                           decomposes into a direct sum of r copies of H ∗ (B). This decomposition must persist
                                           under the full quantum deformation, parametrized by the full Novikov variables of
                                           P(V ) and the bulk-deformation parameter τ̂ ∈ H ∗ (P(V )). The question then arises
                                           as to whether each summand is isomorphic to the quantum cohomology of B. This
                                           paper aims to affirm this correspondence, i.e. QH ∗ (P(V )) decomposes into a direct
                                           sum of r copies of QH ∗ (B). Furthermore, we show that the decomposition can be
                                           lifted to quantum cohomology D-modules.
                                           1.1. Mirror theorem for non-split projective bundles. When the vector bun-
                                           dle V → B is split, i.e. a direct sum of line bundles, the Elezi-Brown mirror theorem
                                           [13, 3] enables us to determine the quantum cohomology of P(V ). This theorem
                                           provides an explicit hypergeometric series, called the I-function, that lies in the
                                           Givental cone of P(V ). Recall that the Givental cone of a smooth projective va-
                                           riety X is a Lagrangian submanifold in the infinite dimensional symplectic vector
                                           space HX = H ∗ (X)((z −1 ))[[Q]]. It encodes all genus-zero Gromov-Witten invariants

Remark 1.4. The quantum Riemann-Roch theorem of Coates-Givental [7] deter-
mines the S 1 -equivariant J-function JVλ from the genus-zero Gromov-Witten invari-
ants of the base B and the Chern classes ci (V ). Therefore Theorem 1.1 determines
all genus-zero Gromov-Witten invariants of P(V ) from those of the base B. This
recovers the genus-zero part of the results of Fan-Lee [16] and Fan [15], who showed
that all genus1 Gromov-Witten invariants of P(V ) can be reconstructed from those
of the base B and the Chern classes ci (V ). On the other hand, in contrast to the
split case, it is not easy to find a closed formula for JVλ in general. We give a non-split
example, the cotangent bundle of Pn , in Example 3.6.

of non-split projective bundles. Strangeway [52] provided a reconstruction algorithm
for certain projective bundles over Pn . The quantum splitting principle of Lee-
Lin-Qu-Wang [39] proposes a reduction to the split case by blowing up the base.

1.2. Decomposition of quantum D-modules. An important property of quan-
tum cohomology is that it forms a D-module over the parameter space. Roughly
speaking, the quantum D-module QDM(X) of a smooth projective variety X is the
module H ∗ (X)[z][[Q, τ ]] equipped with the flat covariant derivatives ∇τ i , ∇z∂z , ∇ξQ∂Q
with respect to the parameters τ , z, Q (see §2.2). Here τ ∈ H ∗ (X) represents the
bulk parameter and Q denotes the Novikov variable. These covariant derivatives,
called the quantum connection, are defined in terms of the quantum multiplication.
The quantum D-module is further equipped with a z-sesquilinear pairing P , induced
from the Poincaré pairing and compatible with the quantum connection. The I- or
J-functions appearing in the previous section are cohomology-valued solutions of
the quantum D-module.
   The Mirror Theorem 1.1 establishes that the quantum D-module QDM(P(V ))
is the Fourier transform of the S 1 -equivariant quantum D-module QDMS 1 (V ) (see
Theorem 1.12 below). By combining this result with the quantum Riemann-Roch
(QRR) theorem of Coates-Givental [7], which connects the Gromov-Witten invari-
ants of V and B, we prove that QDM(P(V )) decomposes into a direct sum of r
copies of QDM(B) after an appropriate localization.

ring of B into a localization of the Novikov ring of P(V ). Let (q, Q) denote the
Novikov variable of P(V ), where q corresponds to the class of a vertical line and Q
is the Novikov variable of the base B, identified via the splitting in Notation 3.1.

1.3. Equivariant quantum cohomology and Fourier transformation. We ex-
plain the idea behind our construction of the I-function. Following the conjecture of
Teleman [53] on quantum cohomology of symplectic reductions, we view the quan-
tum D-module of P(V ) as a Fourier transform of the equivariant quantum D-module
of V . We refer to [29, 32] for a more general conjecture along this line of thought.
   Let (M, ω) be a sympletic manifold equipped with a Hamiltonian S 1 -action, and
let µ : M → R be a moment map. We denote by M//t S 1 the symplectic reduction
µ−1 (t)/S 1 at the level t ∈ R. The equivariant volume of M can be written as a
Fourier transform of the volume of M//t S 1 :

1.4. Plan of the paper. In §2, we review Gromov-Witten invariants, Givental for-
malism and shift operators. We prove a technical Lemma 2.8 on miniveral elements
of the Givental cone, which plays a crucial role in the proof of the mirror theorem.
In §3, we prove our mirror theorem and give examples. In §4, we reformulate the
mirror theorem as a Fourier duality between quantum D-modules of V and P(V ).
In §5, we prove a decomposition of the quantum D-module of P(V ).

Corollary 1.8.
   Corollary 1.8 can be applied to show the semisimplicity of big quantum coho-

variety consisting of flags V1 ⊂ V2 ⊂ · · · ⊂ Vl ⊂ Cn with dim Vi = ki . Fix a
non-degenerate skew-symmetric 2-form ω on C2n . Let IF l(k1 , . . . , kl ; 2n) denote the
isotropic partial flag variety consisting of flags V1 ⊂ V2 ⊂ · · · ⊂ Vl ⊂ C2n such that

kl ≤ n. The varieties G(k, n) = F l(k; n) or IG(k, 2n) = IF l(k; 2n) are also known
as ordinary or isotropic Grassmannians. We have the following result.
Proposition 5.10. The partial flag varieties F l(k1 , . . . , kl ; n) and IF l(k1 , . . . , kl ; 2n)
have generically semisimple big quantum cohomology.
