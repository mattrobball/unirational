## Exact construction

After the recorded projective coordinate change, a line in the selected
Grassmannian chart is written

```text
[S:T:u0*S+u1*T:u2*S+u3*T].
```

Restricting the specialized cubic and equating the four coefficients of
`S^3,S^2*T,S*T^2,T^3` gives an ideal in
`F_p[u0,u1,u2,u3]`.  For every row of the table, Singular verifies:

1. the projective gradient ideal of the cubic is the unit ideal on all four
   standard charts, so the surface is smooth;
2. the Grassmann-chart quotient has vector-space dimension 27;
3. elimination to `F_p[u3]` gives one monic polynomial of degree 27;
4. that polynomial is squarefree.

The eliminant is the minimal polynomial of `u3`.  Its degree equals the full
quotient dimension, so `u3` generates the line algebra.  Squarefreeness makes
that algebra reduced.  A smooth cubic surface has exactly 27 geometric lines,
so this chart contains the complete line scheme rather than a 27-point
subscheme of a larger one.  The irreducible factor degrees are therefore the
exact Frobenius orbit lengths on the 27 lines.

The verifier does not import the producer.  It reconstructs the line
equations with a separate dictionary-polynomial expansion, reruns the
Groebner and elimination calculations, and obtains the factor degrees by a
pure finite-field distinct-degree algorithm.  It also enumerates the
51,840-element Weyl group from the six simple reflections on the 27 standard
line classes.  All five observed cycle types occur in that action.

## Monodromy scope

Unconditionally and for each characteristic separately, the arithmetic
monodromy of the line cover over the smooth `q`-locus in characteristic 23
contains elements of the first two displayed types, and the analogous group
in characteristic 67 contains elements of the last three types.  The
repeated `p=23` type occurs at two separate rational base points.

These are conjugacy-type constraints, not labelled generators.  The
calculation does not provide a simultaneous identification of the 27 lines
between distinct fibres or primes, reconstruct the Schlaefli graph, compute
the subgroup generated by geometric inertia, or prove full `W(E6)`.
Transporting the modular Frobenius elements to one characteristic-zero
arithmetic generic group requires a verified common integral 27-line cover
and its specialization maps.  Identifying them with elements of the
characteristic-zero geometric group needs a further constant-field/geometric
argument.  Neither bridge is asserted here.  The generic line monodromy,
algebraic Brauer group, rational point, and rational section remain unresolved.
