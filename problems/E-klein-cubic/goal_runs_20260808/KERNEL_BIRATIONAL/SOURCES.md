# Primary sources and scope

* T. de Fernex, *On planar Cremona maps of prime order*, Nagoya Math. J.
  **174** (2004), 1--28.  The classification implies that prime-order
  elements of order greater than five are linearizable.
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0302175>
* I. Dolgachev and V. Iskovskikh, *Finite subgroups of the plane Cremona
  group*, in *Algebra, Arithmetic, and Geometry*, Progress in Mathematics
  **269** (2009), 443--548.  Used for the equivariant surface-MMP list.
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0610595>
* I. Dolgachev and V. Iskovskikh, *On elements of prime order in the plane
  Cremona group over a perfect field*, IMRN 2009, 3467--3485.  Its main
  theorem places prime-order elements at least seven on two-dimensional
  tori, but concerns rational points of constant finite groups; it does not
  classify the nonconstant group-scheme cocycle here.
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/0707.4305>
* I. Cheltsov, Yu. Tschinkel, and Zh. Zhang, *Conjugacy in the plane Cremona
  group*, preprint dated 2025-08-13.  The introduction records that linear
  actions of finite cyclic groups are Cremona-conjugate and distinguishes
  conjugacy from the existence of finite-order normalizer lifts.
  <https://math.nyu.edu/~tschinke/papers/yuri/25linearCremona/linear-p2.pdf>
* Z. Reichstein and B. Youssin, *A birational invariant for algebraic group
  actions*, Pacific J. Math. **204** (2002), 223--246.  The cyclic linear
  conjugacy statement used by the preceding source traces to its Theorem
  7.1.
  <https://projecteuclid.org/journals/pacific-journal-of-mathematics/volume-204/issue-1/A-birational-invariant-for-algebraic-group-actions/pjm/1102711030.full>
* G. Bayarmagnai, *Essential dimension of some twists of `mu_(p^n)`*, RIMS
  Kôkyûroku Bessatsu **B4** (2007), 145--151.  Her odd-prime formula predicts
  `ed_K(A)=4`; only its upper bound is proved there.
  <https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/~kenkyubu/bessatsu/open/B4/pdf/B04_010.pdf>
* N. Konovalov, *On the automorphism groups of smooth Fano threefolds*,
  arXiv:2406.03584, Theorems A and B and Remark 7.23.  Used only for the
  prime divisors of finite automorphism groups in the smooth geometric
  Picard-rank-one classification.
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03584>
* A. Kuznetsov, Yu. Prokhorov, and C. Shramov, *Hilbert schemes of lines and
  conics and automorphism groups of Fano threefolds*, Jpn. J. Math. **13**
  (2018), 109--185, Lemma 4.3.4, Corollary 4.3.5, Proposition B.1.5, and
  Proposition B.6.1.  Used for the faithful genus-twelve action on a
  projective plane and the genus-eight Pfaffian cubic construction.
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.02010>
* O. Debarre and G. Mongardi, *Gushel--Mukai varieties with many symmetries
  and an explicit irrational Gushel--Mukai threefold*, Boll. Unione Mat.
  Ital. **15** (2022), 133--161, Section 4.2.2 and Corollary 4.4.  Used for
  the unique ordinary genus-six order-eleven model and its automorphism group
  `C11`.
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.01959>
* X. Roulleau, *The Fano surface of the Klein cubic threefold*, J. Math.
  Kyoto Univ. **49** (2009), 113--129, Proposition 2.1.  Used for uniqueness
  of the smooth cubic threefold admitting an automorphism of order `11`.
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/1001.4853>
* I. Cheltsov, I. Krylov, and S. Ma'u, *G-birationally rigid cubic
  threefolds*, arXiv:2604.20426, Theorem 12 and Lemma 13.  Used for the
  explicit `C11:C5`-equivariant birational link from the Klein cubic to a
  smooth genus-eight Fano threefold of degree `14`.
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20426>
* Yu. Prokhorov, *G-Fano threefolds, II*, Adv. Geom. **13** (2013),
  419--434, Theorem 1.2 and Theorem 6.5.  Used only for the eight-type
  reduction of terminal Gorenstein `G`-Fanos with geometric Picard rank
  greater than one.
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/1101.3854>
* Yu. Prokhorov, *Equivariant minimal model program*, Russian Math. Surveys
  **76** (2021), 461--542, Sections 11.6 and 12.  Used to delimit the
  absence of a complete singular rank-one Gorenstein or non-Gorenstein
  classification; the later addendum gives only necessary finite sieves.
  <https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/rm9990>
* Yu. Prokhorov and C. Shramov, *p-subgroups in the space Cremona group*,
  Math. Nachr. **291** (2018), 1374--1389, Lemmas 7.3--7.7,
  Corollary 7.12, and Proposition 8.1.  Lemmas 7.3--7.5 collect the terminal
  low-index and low-genus anticanonical models.  Lemma 7.6 gives the genus `6`--`10`
  `Q`-factoriality used in the rank-one sieve.  The non-Gorenstein fixed
  singular-point argument in Proposition 8.1 starts at `p>=17`, and
  therefore does not itself dispose of the prime `11` branch.
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02990>
* Yu. Prokhorov, *On G-Fano threefolds*, Izv. Math. **79** (2015),
  795--808, Theorems 2.2 and 2.3.  Used for the terminal Gorenstein
  main-series genus list, anticanonical models, and the warning that the
  geometric and arithmetic `G`-Fano formulations must be distinguished.
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.07873>
* Yu. Prokhorov, *On the number of singular points of factorial terminal
  Fano threefolds*, Math. Notes **101** (2017), 1068--1073, Theorem 1.
  Used for the exact bounds `7,5,3,2,0` in genera `7,8,9,10,12`, and for
  the conditional genus-six `cA_1` bound.
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.10778>
* P. Jahnke and I. Radloff, *Terminal Fano threefolds and their smoothings*,
  Math. Z. **269** (2011), 1129--1136, Theorem 1.4.  Used only for Picard
  constancy in an ordinary smoothing; it supplies no equivariant smoothing
  for the `C11` action or its semilinear normalizer.
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0601769>
* V. V. Nikulin, *Finite automorphism groups of Kahler K3 surfaces*,
  Trans. Moscow Math. Soc. **38** (1980), 71--135.  Used for the fact that
  a symplectic K3 automorphism cannot have order `11`.
* M. Reid, *Young person's guide to canonical singularities*, in
  *Algebraic Geometry, Bowdoin 1985*, Proc. Sympos. Pure Math. **46**, Part
  1 (1987), 345--414.  Used for the index-one cover and cDV description of
  terminal threefold singularities.
* A. Bayer, A. Kuznetsov, and E. Macri, *Mukai models of Fano varieties*,
  Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 6.10.  Used for the canonical genus-six,
  genus-seven, and genus-eight models of factorial terminal prime Fano
  threefolds, including uniqueness of their Mukai data.
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16157>
* H. Dao, *Picard groups of punctured spectra of dimension three local
  hypersurfaces are torsion-free*, J. Algebraic Geom. **21** (2012),
  199--211.  Used to pass from local `Q`-factoriality to factoriality for
  terminal Gorenstein threefold germs before applying the Bayer--Kuznetsov--
  Macri theorem.
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.0471>

The nonexistence of `F55` in `Cr2`, the toric cocycle exclusion, the explicit
monomial conjugator, the arithmetic equivariant-MMP surface exclusion, the
smooth rank-one Fano sieve, and the Klein fixed-point computation are proved
in this packet; they are not computational literature inferences.
