# Fixed-point and tangent reduction for four Laurent terms

**Date:** 2026-08-08  
**Result:** `F55-TRACE-FOUR-TERM-FIXED-POINT-TANGENT-REDUCTION`  
**Headline:** the four-term and global questions remain open

Let

\[
 a=\sum_{j=0}^3 A_j\chi^{s_j},\qquad A_0A_1A_2A_3\ne0,
\]

with four distinct `s_j in M`, and suppose hypothetically that

\[
 \Phi(a)=\sum_{i=0}^4\sigma^i(ca^2\sigma(a))=0.
\]

This note gives an all-exponent structural reduction.  It uses no collision
enumeration.

## 1. The five fixed torus points

The `sigma`-fixed points of the product-one torus are

\[
 y_t=(t,t,t,t,t),\qquad t^5=1.
\]

For `s` represented by `(n_0,...,n_4)`, put

\[
 \delta(s)=\sum_i n_i\pmod5.
\]

This is well defined on `M`, because changing the representative by the
diagonal changes the sum by a multiple of five.

At `y_t`, all five conjugates of `a` have the common value

\[
 a(y_t)=\sum_j A_jt^{\delta(s_j)},
\]

and all five conjugates of `c` have the common nonzero value `t^{-1}`.
Therefore

\[
 0=\Phi(a)(y_t)=5t^{-1}a(y_t)^3,
\]

so `a(y_t)=0` for every fifth root `t`.  Fourier inversion on `Z/5` gives

\[
 \boxed{\ \sum_{j:\delta(s_j)=d}A_j=0\quad\text{for every }d\in\mathbf Z/5.\ }
\tag{1.1}
\]

Because every `A_j` is nonzero, the nonempty residue blocks have size at
least two.  For four terms there are exactly two possibilities:

1. all four exponents have one common residue and `sum A_j=0`; or
2. there are two residue blocks of size two, and the two coefficients in
   each block are opposite.

Thus the partitions `3+1`, `2+1+1`, and `1+1+1+1` are impossible without
examining a single collision hyperplane.

## 2. The cubic tangent tensor

Let `V=M tensor C` and decompose it into the four nontrivial eigenspaces

\[
 V=V_1\oplus V_2\oplus V_3\oplus V_4,
 \qquad \sigma|_{V_q}=\zeta^q.
\]

Near a fixed point `y_t`, the vanishing of `a(y_t)` makes the first
nontrivial jet of the trace cubic its degree-three tangent tensor.  Put

\[
 v_t=\sum_j A_jt^{\delta(s_j)}s_j\in V.
\]

If `L_i` is the linear form associated with `sigma^i(v_t)`, the cubic jet is

\[
 T(v_t)=\sum_{i=0}^4L_i^2L_{i+1}.                         \tag{2.1}
\]

Write `v_t=v_1+v_2+v_3+v_4` in eigencomponents.  Exact character
polarization shows that the only monomials in (2.1) are, up to the common
nonzero factor five,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 &(2\zeta+\zeta^3)v_1^2v_3,\\
 &(\zeta+2\zeta^2)v_1v_2^2,\\
 &(\zeta^2+2\zeta^4)v_2v_4^2,\\
 &(2\zeta^3+\zeta^4)v_3^2v_4.
\end{aligned}                                                   \tag{2.2}
\]

All four cyclotomic coefficients are nonzero.  Hence, set-theoretically,

\[
 \boxed{\ T(v)=0\iff
 v\in U_{14}\cup U_{23},\quad
 U_{14}=V_1\oplus V_4,\quad U_{23}=V_2\oplus V_3.\ }
\tag{2.3}
\]

The two planes are complementary.  Neither contains a nonzero rational
lattice vector.  Indeed the Galois automorphism `zeta -> zeta^2` exchanges
`U_14` and `U_23`; a rational vector in one would lie in their zero
intersection.

## 3. Two residue pairs force two parallel segments

Suppose the residue partition is `2+2`.  Relabel so that

\[
 \delta(s_0)=\delta(s_1)=d,\qquad
 \delta(s_2)=\delta(s_3)=e\ne d.
\]

By (1.1), write

\[
 (A_0,A_1,A_2,A_3)=(B,-B,C,-C).
\]

Put

\[
 u=B(s_0-s_1),\qquad w=C(s_2-s_3),\qquad m=e-d\ne0\pmod5.
\]

Then, up to the harmless scalar `t^d`,

\[
 v_t=u+t^m w.                                             \tag{3.1}
\]

By (2.3), all five vectors (3.1) lie in `U_14 union U_23`.  If `u,w` were
linearly independent, their five projective points would be distinct on the
projective line `P(span(u,w))`.  A projective line not equal to either of the
disjoint lines `P(U_14)` and `P(U_23)` meets their union in at most two
points.  Equality with one of them is also impossible, because `u` and `w`
are nonzero scalar multiples of rational lattice vectors, while neither
spectral plane contains such a vector.

Therefore `u` and `w` are dependent.  Since both underlying differences are
rational lattice vectors,

\[
 \boxed{\ s_0-s_1\text{ and }s_2-s_3\text{ are rationally parallel}.\ }
\tag{3.2}
\]

The four support points are consequently contained in the affine plane
generated by one pair direction and the displacement between the pairs.
Thus a two-residue four-term trace zero, if one exists, is necessarily a pair
of parallel segments; it is never an affinely independent tetrahedron.

This is a necessary condition, not a construction and not an exclusion of
all planar four-point supports.

## 4. Exact remaining tetrahedral branch

Assume now that the four support points are affinely independent over `Q`.
Section 3 excludes the `2+2` residue case.  Hence every hypothetical
tetrahedral trace zero satisfies

\[
 \delta(s_0)=\delta(s_1)=\delta(s_2)=\delta(s_3),
 \qquad \sum_jA_j=0.                                      \tag{4.1}
\]

Put `v=sum A_j s_j`.  Affine independence makes the map

\[
 \{(A_j):\sum A_j=0\}\longrightarrow
 H_{\mathbf C},\qquad (A_j)\longmapsto\sum A_js_j,
\]

an isomorphism, where `H_Q` is the three-dimensional rational span of the
support differences.  By (2.3), `v` lies in `U_14` or `U_23`.

Moreover

\[
 \dim(H_{\mathbf C}\cap U_{14})=
 \dim(H_{\mathbf C}\cap U_{23})=1.                       \tag{4.2}
\]

The dimension is at least one by the dimension formula.  If, for example,
`H_C` contained all of `U_14`, Galois stability of the rational plane `H`
would make it contain `U_23` as well, hence all four dimensions of `V`, a
contradiction.

Consequently, for each fixed affinely independent support satisfying (4.1),
the fixed-point and cubic-jet equations leave **at most two projective
coefficient vectors**: one spectral ray from `H_C intersect U_14`, and one
from `H_C intersect U_23`.  A ray is discarded if one of its four
coefficients is zero.

This is a finite coefficient reduction for every supplied tetrahedral
support, but the supports themselves still form an unbounded lattice family.
Higher jets or full collision classes must exclude or realize those two rays.
No support bound or finite list of lattice tetrahedra follows from the present
argument.

## 5. Verdict

The all-exponent reduction is:

```text
two degree residues  => opposite coefficient pairs + parallel pair differences
affine tetrahedron   => one degree residue + at most two spectral coefficient rays
```

It sharply reduces the four-term problem without touching the 3900 raw
collision equations.  It does not decide the planar same-residue branch, the
two-parallel-segment coefficient systems, or the two spectral rays for every
same-residue lattice tetrahedron.  Therefore:
