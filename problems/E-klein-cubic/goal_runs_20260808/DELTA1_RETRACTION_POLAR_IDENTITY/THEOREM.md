# The degree-one retraction identity and its exact nonsquare boundary

**Date:** 2026-08-08  
**Field:** an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero  
**Group in the Klein application:**
\(G=\operatorname {PSL}_2(\mathbf F_{11})\)  
**Verdict:** the polarized identities and the residual-quadratic descent are
exact; the surviving nonsquare branch is unbounded and is not contradictory

Let

\[
 X=V(F)\subset \mathbf P(W),\qquad \dim W=5,
\]

be the smooth Klein cubic, and let \(\Phi\) be the symmetric trilinear
polarization normalized by

\[
 \Phi(x,x,x)=F(x).
\]

Assume that a primitive homogeneous \(G\)-covariant

\[
 T=(T_0,\ldots,T_4):W\longrightarrow W,
 \qquad \deg T=d,
\]

satisfies

\[
 F(T)=0,\qquad [T]|_X=\operatorname{id}_X.
\tag{0.1}
\]

This is the polynomial form of the degree-one branch of
`FULL_G_RESTRICTION_DOMINANCE`, after composing with the inverse of the
restriction automorphism.

## 1. Exact retraction normal form

**Theorem 1.1.**  There are a homogeneous invariant \(H\) and a homogeneous
covariant \(Q\) such that

\[
 \boxed{\quad T=Hx+FQ,\quad}
\tag{1.1}
\]

with

\[
 \deg H=d-1,\qquad \deg Q=d-3.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Moreover,

\[
 \gcd(H,F)=1.
\tag{1.3}
\]

**Proof.**  On \(X\), the two tuples \(T\) and \(x\) define the same
projective map.  Their proportionality scalar is a section of
\(\mathcal O_X(d-1)\).  Projective normality of the cubic hypersurface lifts
that section to a polynomial \(H\) of degree \(d-1\).  Averaging makes the
lift invariant; the difference from the original lift is a multiple of the
invariant equation \(F\).  Each coordinate of \(T-Hx\) vanishes on the
irreducible hypersurface \(X\), hence is divisible by \(F\), giving (1.1).
Equivariance of \(Q\) follows from equivariance of the other terms.

If \(F\mid H\), then every coordinate of (1.1) is divisible by \(F\),
contrary to primitivity.  Since \(F\) is irreducible, this proves (1.3).
\(\square\)

The presentation has the harmless gauge freedom

\[
 (H,Q)\longmapsto(H+FU,Q-Ux),
 \qquad U\in \mathbf C[W]^G_{d-4}.
\tag{1.4}
\]

## 2. The complete polarized system

Put

\[
 A=\Phi(x,x,Q),\qquad
 B=\Phi(x,Q,Q),\qquad
 C=F(Q).
\tag{2.1}
\]

Expansion of \(F(Hx+FQ)=0\), followed by cancellation of the nonzero
polynomial \(F\), gives

\[
 H^3+3H^2A+3HFB+F^2C=0.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Reducing (2.2) modulo \(F\) and using (1.3) gives the first requested exact
identity:

\[
 \boxed{\quad H+3\Phi(x,x,Q)=FR\quad}
\tag{2.3}
\]

for an invariant \(R\) of degree \(d-4\).  Substitution in (2.2) gives

\[
 H^2R+3HB+FC=0.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Reduction modulo \(H\), again using \(\gcd(H,F)=1\), gives the requested
divisibility and its quotient:

\[
 \boxed{\quad H\mid F(Q),\qquad F(Q)=HS\quad}
\tag{2.5}
\]

with invariant \(S\) of degree \(2d-8\).  Cancelling \(H\) in (2.4) yields
the third identity

\[
 \boxed{\quad HR+3\Phi(x,Q,Q)+FS=0.\quad}
\tag{2.6}
\]

These three identities are equivalent to the following factorization of the
entire cubic cut out on the line in direction \(Q(x)\):

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 F(x+tQ)=(Ht-F)(St^2-Rt-1).
 \quad}
\tag{2.7}
\]

Thus the retraction does not merely select one point of the cubic; it splits
off one root from a residual quadratic.

## 3. Exact smaller-map dichotomy

Let

\[
 \Delta=R^2+4S\in \mathbf C[W]^G_{2d-8}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

If \(\Delta=D^2\), put

\[
 U_+=\frac{R+D}{2}x-Q,
 \qquad
 U_-=\frac{D-R}{2}x+Q.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Then both tuples have degree \(d-3\) and

\[
 F(U_+)=F(U_-)=0.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Indeed,

\[
 St^2-Rt-1=
 \left(\frac{R+D}{2}t+1\right)
 \left(\frac{D-R}{2}t-1\right),
\tag{3.4}
\]

and (3.3) follows from (2.7) after clearing the two denominators.  Any
polynomial common factor of either tuple can be cancelled, producing an
even smaller primitive landing covariant.  Because \(G\) is perfect, the
cancelled factor is invariant and equivariance survives.

Conversely, the residual quadratic splits over \(\mathbf C(W)\) exactly when
\(\Delta\) is a square.  Since the polynomial ring is a UFD and constants in
\(\mathbf C\) are squares, a polynomial \(\Delta\) which is a square in the
fraction field is already a polynomial square.

Consequently:

* a square discriminant gives an exact degree-lowering landing map;
* a globally minimal landing covariant whose restriction is the identity
  must have \(\Delta\) nonsquare;
* minimality only among **retractions** is weaker: the maps (3.2) need not
  restrict with degree one, so they are smaller landing maps, not
  automatically smaller retractions.

There is no invariant-degree reason for \(\Delta\) to be a square.  The
certified primary invariants include algebraically independent forms
\(f_3=F\) and \(f_5\).  For every \(d\ge36\), write

\[
 d-8=3a+5b
\]

(every integer at least eight is in the semigroup generated by 3 and 5) and
put \(J=f_3^a f_5^b\).  Then

\[
 f_3f_5J^2
\]

is an invariant nonsquare of the required degree \(2d-8\): its
\(f_3\)-valuation is odd.  This does not construct \(Q\); it proves that
degree, invariance, and factor parity cannot turn (3.1) into a finite square
test.

## 4. The base divisor and the six-line incidence cover

Let

\[
 B=V(F,H)\subset X.
\tag{4.1}
\]

At every generic point of a component of \(B\), (2.3), (2.5), and (2.6)
give

\[
 F(x)=A=B=C=0.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Hence

\[
 F(ux+vQ(x))=0
\tag{4.3}
\]

for all \([u:v]\).  Equivalently, the first exceptional divisor over the
complete-intersection base has map

\[
 [u:v]\longmapsto[ux+vQ(x)]
\tag{4.4}
\]

and its noncollapsed fibres are lines contained in \(X\).

Let \(\mathcal F(X)\) be the Fano surface of lines and

\[
 e:\mathcal I=\{(\ell,x):x\in\ell\subset X\}\longrightarrow X
\tag{4.5}
\]

the incidence map.  It is generically of degree six: at a general point,
line directions are the transverse intersection of the quadratic and cubic
terms in the tangent \(\mathbf P^2\).  On the open locus where
\(x\wedge Q(x)\ne0\), (4.4) therefore gives a \(G\)-equivariant rational
section

\[
 B\dashrightarrow\mathcal I,\qquad
 x\longmapsto(\langle x,Q(x)\rangle,x)
\tag{4.6}
\]

of this six-sheeted cover over \(B\).

There is a useful but nonterminal canonical-class consequence.  Suppose a
smooth component of \(B\) is cut by an irreducible factor of degree
\(h=d-1\), and the line map

\[
 \ell:B\dashrightarrow\mathcal F(X)
\]

is generically finite.  Its Pluecker coordinates \(x_iQ_j-x_jQ_i\) have
degree \(d-2\).  If their fixed divisor is \(Z\), then

\[
 \ell^*K_{\mathcal F(X)}
 =\mathcal O_B(d-2)(-Z),
\tag{4.7}
\]

because adjunction on \(\operatorname {Gr}(2,5)\) gives
\(K_{\mathcal F(X)}=\mathcal O_{\mathcal F(X)}(1)\).  On the other hand,

\[
 K_B=\mathcal O_B(d-3).
\tag{4.8}
\]

Thus a base-divisor-free generically finite line map is impossible: Hurwitz
would give an effective ramification divisor linearly equivalent to
\(-\mathcal O_B(1)\).  If \(\operatorname {Pic}(B)=\mathbf Z[\mathcal O_B(1)]\)
and \(Z\sim e\mathcal O_B(1)\), the exact formula is

\[
 R_\ell\sim(e-1)\mathcal O_B(1),
\tag{4.9}
\]

so only \(e\ge1\) is forced.  The alternatives are a positive fixed
collinearity divisor, a line-map image of dimension at most one, or the
singular/reducible branch.  None is excluded by the retraction identities.

## 5. Why incidence splitting is not a bounded target

The incidence consequence (4.6), by itself, is abundant in arbitrarily high
divisor class.  Let \(D_0\) be a sufficiently general divisor in a high
very-ample system on the incidence threefold \(\mathcal I\).  On the finite
locus of \(e\), the off-diagonal fibre product

\[
 (\mathcal I\times_X\mathcal I)\setminus\Delta_{\mathcal I}
\]

has dimension three.  Requiring both points to lie in \(D_0\) imposes two
independent divisor conditions, so the double-hit locus has dimension at
most one.  Therefore

\[
 e|_{D_0}:D_0\dashrightarrow e(D_0)
\]

is generically one-to-one.

Taking the union of the finitely many translates \(gD_0\) gives a
\(G\)-stable split divisor, componentwise birational to its image, and its
class grows with the chosen very-ample power.  The image is a \(G\)-stable
divisor on \(X\).  Since \(\operatorname {Pic}(X)=\mathbf Z[\mathcal O_X(1)]\),
projective normality and the absence of nontrivial characters lift its
equation to an invariant ambient polynomial of unbounded degree.

Thus no finite classification of invariant split divisors of (4.5) can
close the retraction branch.  A successful theorem must use the much
stronger fact that the section is represented by one global covariant
\(Q\), with the degree relation (1.2) and all identities (2.3)--(2.6).

## 6. Exact primitive irreducible countermodel with nonsquare \(\Delta\)

The nonsquare boundary is not a formal artifact.  The verifier constructs
the following exact example over \(\mathbf Q\).  Put

\[
 q=z_0^2+z_1^2+z_2^2+z_3^2,
 \qquad
 c=z_0^3+2z_1^3+3z_2^3+5z_3^3,
\]

and

\[
 F_0=qw+c.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The cubic \(F_0\) is irreducible because \(\gcd(q,c)=1\) and it is primitive
and linear in \(w\).  Define

\[
 z'=\bigl(z_0^3,z_0^2z_1+F_0,z_0^2z_2,z_0^2z_3\bigr),
\]

\[
 q'=q(z'),\qquad c'=c(z'),
\]

and the degree-nine tuple

\[
 T_0=(q'z'_0,q'z'_1,q'z'_2,q'z'_3,-c').
\tag{6.2}
\]

Then

\[
 F_0(T_0)=0,qquad
 [T_0]|_{V(F_0)}=\operatorname{id},
\]

with

\[
 H_0=z_0^6q,qquad
 T_0=H_0x+F_0Q_0.
\]

The five coordinates of \(T_0\) have gcd one.  For the associated residual
quadratic, specialization at

\[
 (z_0,z_1,z_2,z_3)=(0,0,0,1)
\]

gives

\[
 \Delta=-4(w+5)^2(w^2+5w-1),
\tag{6.3}
\]

which is not a square.  Hence the global \(\Delta\) is not a square.

This is an actual primitive polynomial retraction onto an irreducible cubic,
not merely a coefficient ledger.  Its target is singular at
\([0:0:0:0:1]\), and it is not \(G\)-equivariant.  It therefore does not
answer the Klein problem.  It proves that primitivity, irreducibility of the
cubic, the complete polarized system, residual factor arithmetic, and
projective idempotence do not by themselves force a contradiction; a proof
must use smooth Klein geometry or the full representation.

## 7. Iteration and exact stop

Substitution of \(F(T)=0\) in (1.1) gives the exact iteration identity

\[
 \boxed{\quad T(T(x))=H(T(x))T(x).\quad}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Also

\[
 H(T)\equiv H^d\pmod F.
\tag{7.2}
\]

After projective saturation, the common factor \(H(T)\) in (7.1) cancels
and returns the original tuple \(T\).  Iteration therefore supplies no
degree descent.  Precomposition by the primitive quartic endomorphism does
not preserve the identity restriction and likewise gives no retraction
descent.

The exact remaining alternatives in the degree-one branch are:

1. prove, using the smooth Klein cubic and the full \(G\)-module structure,
   that \(\Delta\) must be a square, and then control the smaller landing
   maps in (3.2); or
2. exclude the nonsquare residual double cover together with the unbounded
   invariant base-divisor sections in Section 5.

Neither implication is presently proved, and neither is a finite
theorem-forced CAS target.
