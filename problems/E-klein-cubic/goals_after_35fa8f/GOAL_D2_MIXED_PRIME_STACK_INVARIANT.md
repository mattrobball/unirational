# Goal D2 — find a genuinely new mixed-prime quotient-stack invariant

**Pinned state:** `35fa8f59b6a1423cc89300aeaceefe91552be5ba`

## Mission

Search for an integral or mixed-prime invariant of `[X/G]` or of the generic `G`-torsor that is not reproduced by arbitrary free-orbit blowup centres and is functorial under the relative-dimension-one resolution of a hypothetical equivariant map.

Goal D proves that ordinary index-valued degree formulas, rational Hodge/motive summands, and the audited integral cohomology lattice are too flexible in the unrestricted blowup category. This goal must begin with a new invariant and a new bridge, not more computation of the same data.

## Candidate directions

- mod-`p` equivariant Chow motives at `p=2,3,5,11` with extension/gluing data;
- mixed-prime cohomological invariants of the generic `PSL(2,11)` torsor;
- quotient-stack Steenrod or power operations with stabilizer corrections;
- canonical dimension/compression invariants not detected by ordinary index;
- integral polarization plus group action on the intermediate Jacobian;
- equivariant cobordism classes modulo the closure generated by **admissible base-locus centres**, not arbitrary centres.

## Work packages

### D2.0 — theorem and bridge selection

State a precise invariant and prove it is:

- defined for the genuine quotient stack/twist;
- functorial for the resolved relative-dimension-one map or an exact extracted correspondence;
- stable under the actual sequence of blowups;
- not annihilated by the uncontrolled multisection degree;
- sensitive after all twists have index one.

If no such theorem is found, exit before large computation.

### D2.1 — target and source closure

Compute the invariant for `X` and characterize the closure generated from `P(W)` by centres that can actually occur in the base locus of a landing covariant. Goal D's unrestricted free-orbit Prym centre must be tested explicitly.

### D2.2 — contradiction or reproducibility

Prove the target lies outside the admissible closure, or construct an admissible centre system reproducing it and refute the candidate invariant.
