## Good-reduction lemma

Let `M` be a matrix over the fraction field of an integral domain `R`, and
let `m` be a prime of `R` at which every recorded denominator is a unit.  If
an `r x r` minor of the reduction of `M` modulo `m` is nonzero, the same
minor is a nonzero element of `Frac(R)`.  Therefore

```text
rank_Frac(R)(M) >= rank_k(m)(M mod m).
```

This elementary implication is the only use of modular arithmetic in the
emptiness theorem.  It is a nonzero-minor proof in characteristic zero, not
a point count, heuristic, bounded support search, or assertion that the
special fibre equals the generic field.

The producer records, for each class and for both split primes, all input
denominator products, the precise pivot columns, and a nonzero determinant
of the corresponding `5 x 5` and `11 x 11` minors.  Its independent replay
reconstructs the matrices rather than trusting stored ranks.  The
`p=199` computation is an unused holdout; one good prime already proves the
nonvanishing, while the second detects convention or transcription errors.

It follows from the recorded minors that, in characteristic zero,

```text
rank(C_1) = rank(C_2) = 5,
rank(S_1) = rank(S_2) = 11.
```

The upper bounds are the row counts, so the displayed lower bounds are
equalities.
