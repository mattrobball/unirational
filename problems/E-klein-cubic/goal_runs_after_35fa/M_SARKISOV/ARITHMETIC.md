# Fibration arithmetic

Let \(K_f=K(\mathbf P^1)\) and let \(S/K_f\) be the generic cubic-surface
fibre.

## Effective zero-cycles

1. The plane \(u=0\) cuts out \(C_{012,K_f}\). A line in that plane gives
   an effective degree-3 zero-cycle on \(S\).
2. The connected orbit of involution lines projects isomorphically to the
   base and gives one closed point on \(S\) with residue degree 55.

Therefore \(\operatorname{ind}(S)=1\). This is not a rational point.

## Section-or-quartic frontier

Voisin's theorem for smooth cubic surfaces over characteristic-zero fields
says that a zero-cycle of degree one forces either a rational point or a
point over an extension of degree four. Equivalently, the degree-55 point is
already of degree prime to three and meets the hypothesis.

If \(S(K_f)=\varnothing\), the degree-four point cannot have residue degree
one. It cannot have residue degree two: the line through a quadratic conjugate
pair is defined over \(K_f\), and its residual third intersection is a
\(K_f\)-point (or the line is contained in \(S\) and itself has
\(K_f\)-points). Thus the alternative is an integral quartic point and its
closure is a genuine degree-4 multisection.

## Positive bridge

A \(K_f\)-point of \(S\) is a rational section of \(f\). Properness extends
it over the smooth base curve. Evaluating at a \(K\)-rational base point and
composing with \(\pi\) produces a \(K\)-point of \(X_T\), which is the
accepted headline-positive generic-twist output.

The packet does not decide whether the section or quartic branch occurs.
