# Counterexample and nonimplication audit

## 1. Homogeneous normal-image family

For `e>=3`, in source coordinates `(z_1,...,z_4,u)` define

```text
Q = sum_i z_i^(e-1),
B = sum_i z_i^e,
Phi_e = (-B,z_1 Q,...,z_4 Q).
```

Its coordinates are primitive, its generic Jacobian rank is four, and its
normal rational hypersurface image is

```text
H_e: y_0 sum_i y_i^(e-1) + sum_i y_i^e = 0.
```

The pulled-gradient gcd and discrepancy data are

```text
h=Q^(e-2),
a_D=e-2,
beta_D=2,
epsilon_D=1,
A_E(H_e,0)=5-e.
```

The kernel foliation is generated by the constant `u`-direction and is log
canonical.  At `e=5`, the target pair is lc with `A_E=0` and one reduced
copy survives.  For `e>=6`, the defect grows without bound.

What it refutes: any proof based only on primitivity, homogeneity, rank four,
normality, target-pair lc, or foliation lc.

What it does not refute: a theorem using the full `PSL(2,11)` representation
and genuine minimality.

## 2. Fixed plt nodal target with arbitrary pullback support

Take the fixed hypersurface

```text
H_0: v^2=u^2(u+1)
```

with normalization

```text
u=t^2-1,
v=t(t^2-1).
```

For distinct scalars `lambda_i`, let

```text
P_N=product_(i=1)^N (x-lambda_i s),
t=1+P_N.
```

Then the pulled-gradient gcd is `P_N(P_N+2)`.  The `N` reduced divisors
`x-lambda_i s=0` all dominate the same conductor branch, while the target,
normalization, and plt pair remain fixed.

What it refutes: any conductor-support bound derived from target
normalization/plt geometry alone.

What it does not refute: a representation-specific minimal support theorem.

## 3. `P22` logarithmic nonrigidity

The exact orbit-eleven packet constructs primitive homogeneous
`G`-equivariant logarithmic vector fields in degrees at most 25 and 28 that
are tangent to every maximal-`A5` quadric and nonradial modulo `P22`.

What it refutes: equivariance + primitivity + logarithmic tangency as a
minimal-contraction theorem.

What it does not refute: the Jacobian-kernel, first-integral, adjugate, and
minimality conditions of an actual KLS tuple.

## 4. Exit classification

None of these examples is simultaneously a minimal Klein-group KLS tuple.
It would be false to label this packet `KLS2-COUNTEREXAMPLE`.  Their exact
role is to prove that all generic routes to KLS2.1 fail and that a finite
classification cannot be launched from the accepted hypotheses.  The
correct exit is `KLS2-NO-FINITE-REDUCTION`.
