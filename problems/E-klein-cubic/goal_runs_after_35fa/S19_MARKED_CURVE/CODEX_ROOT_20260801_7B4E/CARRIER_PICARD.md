# Special quintic-carrier and liaison boundary

On the epsilon-one locus, the unique quintic is exactly

`S_q=V(F5_h+F3_h*Q2)`, `Q2 in H0(P3,O(2))`.

The identity `(F5_h+F3_h*Q2,F3_h)=(F5_h,F3_h)` shows that the fixed `(3,5)`
curve `Y` is scheme-theoretically `3H` on every such carrier.  It therefore
does not add an independent Picard class.

For a smooth quintic carrier, `K_S=H`.  A degree-19 arithmetic-genus-zero
divisor must satisfy

`C.H=19`, `C^2+C.H=-2`, hence `C^2=-21`.

If `Pic(S_q)=Z H`, degree is a multiple of `H^2=5`, so degree 19 is
impossible.  This is conditional only.  The actual carrier would be selected
by an unknown point of the epsilon-one incidence locus, and its Picard or
class group has not been determined.  A very-general statement would not
exclude this special locus.  The inherited base-locus Noether--Lefschetz
hypothesis also fails because `I_Y(4)=F3_h*S1` is not globally generated.

For a proper `(5,s)` link, the residual degrees and genera begin

| s | residual degree | residual arithmetic genus |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 6 | 11 | -28 |
| 7 | 16 | -12 |
| 8 | 21 | 9 |

The negative values exclude reduced connected residuals only.  They do not
exclude disconnected or nonreduced residuals, and they do not close the
epsilon-one branch.

Therefore S19.2 remains undecided at the exact point requested by the goal:
the Picard lattice of the curve-selected special carrier cannot be computed
until such a carrier is selected, or the entire saturated epsilon-one
incidence projection is eliminated.
