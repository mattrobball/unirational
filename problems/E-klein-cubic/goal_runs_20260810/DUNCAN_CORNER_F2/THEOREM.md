## 1. Proposition A — there is only one flavour of fabulous corner

> **Proposition A.** Let `G = PSL(2,11)` act faithfully on a smooth projective
> variety `Z` in toroidal form. A codimension-2 corner `D_i ∩ D_j` is
> **fabulous iff `G_{D_ij} ≅ V4`**, and then
> `{G_{D_i}, G_{D_j}} = {⟨z⟩, ⟨s⟩}` for two **distinct commuting** involutions
> generating that `V4`; the third involution `r = zs` also fixes `D_ij`
> pointwise.

*Proof.* Duncan `def:toroidal`(c) with the commentary at lines 83–90 forces
`G_x` to be abelian of rank `≤ |I(x)|`, so `G_{D_ij}` is abelian of rank `≤ 2`.
By `thm:pairs` (line 728) fabulous ⟺ non-cyclic. **PSL(2,11) has exactly 55
non-cyclic abelian subgroups and every one is a Klein four-group** — verified by
exhaustive enumeration of every subgroup generated by a commuting pair over all
660 matrices, at two split primes (`w3_corner_inventory.py`, PART 1:
*"non-cyclic abelian subgroups: 55, orders [4]"*). Duncan lines 723–725 give
`G_{D_i} = ker χ_2`, `G_{D_j} = ker χ_1` with `χ_1 ≠ χ_2` generating `V̂4`,
hence the two kernels are distinct order-2 subgroups. ∎

Two immediate corollaries, both machine-checked:

* **No corner involving a `C3`/`C5`/`C6`/`C11`-divisor is ever fabulous**: the
  element orders occurring in non-cyclic abelian subgroups of `PSL(2,11)` are
  exactly `{1,2}`.
* For any fabulous corner, `Stab_G(D_i) ∩ Stab_G(D_j) = V4` (order 4): such an
  element normalizes `V` and fixes both `s` and `z`, so it lies in
  `C_G(z) ∩ C_G(s) = V` (W6: *"|C_G(z) ∩ C_G(s)| … {4}"*). This caps how much
  the Duncan-style "coincidence point is `⟨Stab, Stab⟩`-fixed" endgame can ever
  deliver here.

## 7. Theorem E, and why nothing closes

> **Theorem E (target-side receiver theorem for the Klein cubic).** Let `Y` be
> any smooth projective `G`-variety in stabilizer-stratified toroidal form with
> a fabulous corner `D_ij` (necessarily `G_{D_ij} = V = ⟨z,s⟩` by Proposition A)
> and `D_ij` rationally connected. For every `G`-equivariant rational
> `f : Y ⇢ X`:
> 1. `F_{(i,j)} ⊆ closure f(D_i) ⊆ X^s` and `F_{(j,i)} ⊆ closure f(D_j) ⊆ X^z`;
> 2. both lie in one connected RCC `T ⊆ X_nt`, and by Proposition D `T` is a
>    point or a connected union of the 55 lines;
> 3. hence **either** both flag limits lie in the 55-line configuration and are
>    joined by a chain of lines there, **or** both collapse to a single point of
>    `X^V` (6 points: 3 type-I, 3 type-II).

> **No closure at `|I| = 2`.** Two independent structural escapes.
>
> **Escape 1.** At *every* fabulous corner `z` and `s` **commute**
> (Proposition A) — and precisely for commuting involutions `L_z ∩ L_s ≠ ∅`
> (W5: edges = commuting pairs). So the default landing "each divisor sweeps its
> own line", which (F1) already *forces* for the `D12`-stabilized divisors, is
> automatically chain-consistent, via the shortest possible chain: two lines
> meeting at a type-I vertex of the `V`-triangle.
>
> **Escape 2.** Even in the fully-collapsed branch, the three type-II points of
> a `V`-triangle lie on **all three** elliptics `E_z, E_s, E_r`, so "both
> divisors contract to the same type-II point" is available whenever both
> setwise stabilizers lie in `V`.

Duncan's own `S_4`-on-`P²` endgame (`thm:no_map_to_dp2`, line 1275; remark at
1409–1427) works because `S_nt` contains **no** rational curve. Here `X_nt` is

`DUNCAN-NO-CLOSURE-AT-I2`.
