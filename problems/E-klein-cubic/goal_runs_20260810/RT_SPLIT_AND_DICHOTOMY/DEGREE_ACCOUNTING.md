# Exact degree/orbit-size accounting

## 1. Scope of the Bézout bounds

Let `I_A` be generated by a primitive homogeneous landing tuple of degree
`d` on `P4`.  Choose general members successively and, at stage `r`, remove
the fixed components already produced in codimension `<r` (the
Vogel/refined-intersection construction).  The resulting codimension-`c`
cycle is effective, has degree at most `d^c`, and contains every
codimension-`c` base component isolated after the lower-codimension components
are removed, with positive local intersection multiplicity.  Hence
\[
\sum_{\operatorname{codim}Z=c}\operatorname{mult}_Z\deg Z\le d^c.
\tag{1.1}
\]
For the dimensions relevant here:

| base component | dimension | codimension | capacity |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| surface | 2 | 2 | `d^2` |
| curve | 1 | 3 | `d^3` |
| residual isolated point scheme | 0 | 4 | `d^4` |

For the curve and point rows, `component` means a component remaining after
lower-codimension base components are removed; the point row is the residual
zero-dimensional scheme.  These are the components controlled by the
effective Vogel cycles.  Equation (1.1) does not count a strict-support
stratum that is merely contained in a larger base component.

## 2. Orbit sizes

`|G|=660`.  The maximal-subgroup indices producing the smallest possible
nontrivial orbit lower bounds are
\[
11,\ 11,\ 12,\ 55,\ 66,
\]
and a free orbit has size `660`.

For an orbit of `n` distinct degree-at-least-one components of codimension
`c`, the first degree at which the crude capacity allows the orbit is
\[
d_{\min}=\min\{d:n\le d^c\}.
\]
