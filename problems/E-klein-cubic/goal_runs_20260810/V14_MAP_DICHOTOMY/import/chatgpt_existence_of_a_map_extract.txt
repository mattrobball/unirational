## The map exists

Let

\[
G=\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbf F_{11}),\qquad
V=V_{14},\qquad
C=K_{\mathrm{Kl}}
\]

with their standard actions. Then

\[
\boxed{\text{there exists a nonconstant }G\text{-equivariant rational map }
\Phi:V\dashrightarrow C.}
\]

The construction uses the order-two Schur obstruction rather than trying to eliminate it. A \(V_{14}\)-point reduces that obstruction to a quaternion algebra; after a quadratic splitting, the ordinary Pfaffian–Grassmannian map produces a quadratic point of the cubic; the cubic secant construction then descends it to a rational point.

Tschinkel–Zhang supply the geometric input: \(C\) and \(V\) are the Pfaffian–Grassmannian pair associated with the six-dimensional representation \(U\) of \(\widetilde G=\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf F_{11})\). Once \(\mathbf P(U)\) is split, choosing a hyperplane gives the classical birational map between the cubic and \(V_{14}\). Globally they prove

\[
C\times\mathbf P^2\times\mathbf P(U)
\sim_G
V\times\mathbf P^2\times\mathbf P(U),
\]

where the action on \(\mathbf P(U)\) is projectively linear. citeturn157818view1

---

## 1. Pass to the generic twist of \(V_{14}\)

Put

\[
F=\mathbf C(V)^G,\qquad E=\mathbf C(V),
\]

and let

\[
T=\operatorname{Spec}E\longrightarrow \operatorname{Spec}F
\]

be the generic \(G\)-torsor. Write \(Z_T\) for the twist of a \(G\)-variety \(Z\) by \(T\).

The generic point of \(V\) gives a canonical point

\[
v_T\in V_T(F).
\]

Now twist the projective six-dimensional representation:

\[
{}^T\mathbf P(U)=\operatorname{SB}(\mathcal A),
\]

where \(\mathcal A\) is a central simple \(F\)-algebra of degree \(6\).

Because

\[
V\subset \operatorname{Gr}(2,U),
\]

twisting gives

\[
V_T\subset \operatorname{SB}_2(\mathcal A),
\]

where \(\operatorname{SB}_2(\mathcal A)\) parametrizes right ideals of reduced dimension \(2\). Therefore \(v_T\) determines an \(F\)-rational right ideal of reduced dimension \(2\).

For a central simple algebra, the reduced dimensions of right ideals are multiples of the index. Hence

\[
\operatorname{ind}(\mathcal A)\mid 2.
\]

Thus either:

\[
\mathcal A\simeq M_6(F),
\]

or

\[
\mathcal A\simeq M_3(D)
\]

for a quaternion division algebra \(D/F\).

Consequently there is a separable extension

\[
L/F,\qquad [L:F]\leq 2,
\]

such that

\[
\mathcal A_L\simeq M_6(L).
\]

This is the critical point: **the generic \(V_{14}\)-point itself forces the six-dimensional Schur obstruction to split after at most a quadratic extension.**

---

## 2. Over \(L\), use the ordinary Pfaffian–Grassmannian birationality

After passing to \(L\), the twisted projective space becomes

\[
\operatorname{SB}(\mathcal A)_L\simeq \mathbf P^5_L.
\]

Thus the twisted pair

\[
(C_{T,L},V_{T,L})
\]

is now an ordinary Pfaffian cubic and its associated degree-\(14\) Fano threefold over \(L\). The classical Palatini construction gives

\[
V_{T,L}\dashrightarrow C_{T,L}.
\]

Equivalently, choose a general \(L\)-hyperplane

\[
\Pi\subset \mathbf P(U_L);
\]

the hyperplane section of the common Palatini quartic gives birational maps

\[
V_{T,L}\dashrightarrow
\Pi\cap Q_T
\dashrightarrow C_{T,L}.
\]

The existence and canonical nature of these hyperplane maps are precisely the construction underlying the Tschinkel–Zhang stable birationality. citeturn157818view1

Since

\[
v_T\in V_T(F)\subset V_T(L),
\]

and both threefolds are smooth and proper, birational invariance of existence of rational points—Nishimura’s lemma—gives

\[
C_T(L)\neq\varnothing.
\]

Choose

\[
x\in C_T(L).
\]

If \(L=F\), the construction is complete. Assume henceforth that \(L/F\) is quadratic, with nontrivial automorphism \(\iota\).

---

## 3. Descend the quadratic point by the third-intersection operation

The five-dimensional representation defining the Klein cubic is an **honest linear \(G\)-representation**. Therefore its twist remains the projectivization of an \(F\)-vector space:

\[
C_T\subset\mathbf P(W_T)\simeq\mathbf P^4_F.
\]

The two conjugate points

\[
x,\qquad x^\iota
\]

lie on \(C_T\). Their span

\[
\ell=\langle x,x^\iota\rangle
\]

is an \(F\)-defined projective line.

If \(x=x^\iota\), then \(x\in C_T(F)\).

If \(\ell\subset C_T\), then \(\ell\simeq\mathbf P^1_F\), so again

\[
C_T(F)\neq\varnothing.
\]

Otherwise,

\[
\ell\cap C_T=x+x^\iota+r
\]

as a degree-three divisor. Since the first two points form a degree-two Galois-invariant divisor, the residual point satisfies

\[
r\in C_T(F).
\]

This residual point has an explicit formula. Let

\[
\mathcal F\in\operatorname{Sym}^3(W_T^\vee)
\]

be the twisted Klein cubic equation and let \(\mathcal B\) be its symmetric trilinear polarization, normalized by

\[
\mathcal F(z)=\mathcal B(z,z,z).
\]

Put

\[
a=\mathcal B(x,x,x^\iota),\qquad
b=\mathcal B(x,x^\iota,x^\iota)=a^\iota .
\]

Then the third point on the secant is

\[
\boxed{
r=
\left[
b\,x-a\,x^\iota
\right].
}
\]

Indeed,

\[
\mathcal F(sx+tx^\iota)
=
3st\bigl(as+bt\bigr),
\]

so \((s:t)=(b:-a)\) is the third intersection. Moreover,

\[
\left(bx-ax^\iota\right)^\iota
=
ax^\iota-bx
=
-\left(bx-ax^\iota\right),
\]

so its projective class is fixed by \(\iota\) and is therefore \(F\)-rational.

We have proved

\[
\boxed{C_T(F)\neq\varnothing.}
\]

---

## 4. Untwist

The twisting correspondence gives

\[
C_T(F)
\;\simeq\;
\{\text{\(G\)-equivariant \(E\)-points of \(C\)}\}.
\]

An \(E=\mathbf C(V)\)-point of \(C\) is exactly a rational map from \(V\) to \(C\). Thus the point \(r\) constructed above untwists to

\[
\boxed{
\Phi:V_{14}\dashrightarrow K_{\mathrm{Kl}}.
}
\]

At the generic point, one may write it schematically as

\[
\Phi(\eta_V)
=
\operatorname{third}
\left(
\chi_\Pi(v_T),
\chi_{\iota\Pi}(v_T)
\right),
\]

where \(\chi_\Pi\) is an \(L\)-defined Pfaffian–Palatini birational map and “third” means the residual point of the secant line.

The map cannot be constant: a constant \(G\)-equivariant map would land at a \(G\)-fixed point of the Klein cubic, but the irreducible five-dimensional Klein representation has no invariant line.

## Why the construction is one-directional

The mechanism uses two special features of the direction

\[
V_{14}\dashrightarrow C:
\]

\[
V_T(F)\neq\varnothing
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{ind}(\mathcal A)\mid2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
C_T\text{ has a point over a quadratic extension}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
C_T(F)\neq\varnothing.
\]

The last implication is special to a **cubic hypersurface**: two conjugate points have a residual third point. There is no analogous operation converting a quadratic point of \(V_{14}\) into an \(F\)-point. Thus this does not contradict the previously proved nonexistence of

\[
C\dashrightarrow V_{14}.
\]

What is now established is

\[
\boxed{
K_{\mathrm{Kl}}\dashrightarrow V_{14}:\ \text{no map},
\qquad
V_{14}\dashrightarrow K_{\mathrm{Kl}}:\ \text{a nonconstant map exists}.
}
\]

This argument does not by itself determine whether \(\Phi\) is dominant or compute its generic degree.

## Conclusion

Let

\[
G=\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbf F_{11}),\qquad
X=K=\text{the Klein cubic},\qquad
Y=V_{14}=\text{the other Prokhorov threefold}.
\]

Then, using the fixed-locus computations already sealed in the repository,

\[
\boxed{\text{There is no }G\text{-equivariant rational map }
X\dashrightarrow Y.}
\]

This is stronger than nonexistence of a dominant or birational map: **there is no equivariant rational map at all**, even one with lower-dimensional image.

### Proof

Choose an involution \(\sigma\in G\), and put

\[
N=C_G(\sigma)\simeq D_{12}.
\]

On the Klein cubic, the five-dimensional representation decomposes as

\[
W=W_\sigma^+\oplus W_\sigma^-,
\qquad
\dim W_\sigma^+=3,\quad \dim W_\sigma^-=2.
\]

The projective line

\[
L_\sigma=\mathbf P(W_\sigma^-)
\]

lies entirely in \(X\). Since \(\sigma\) acts by \(-1\) on \(W_\sigma^-\), it acts trivially on its projectivization, so \(L_\sigma\subset X^\sigma\) pointwise. Moreover, every element of \(N\) commutes with \(\sigma\), hence preserves \(W_\sigma^-\); consequently \(L_\sigma\) is \(N\)-stable. The repository verifies all of this exactly in characteristic zero. fileciteturn12file0L2-L2

For \(Y=V_{14}\), the corresponding sealed computation gives

\[
Y^\sigma=C_\sigma\sqcup\{p_+,p_-\},
\]

where \(C_\sigma\) is a smooth irreducible genus-one sextic, and also

\[
Y^N=Y^{D_{12}}=\varnothing.
\]

Thus no positive-dimensional component of \(Y^\sigma\) contains a rational curve. fileciteturn5file0L2-L2

Now suppose that

\[
f:X\dashrightarrow Y
\]

were \(G\)-equivariant. Resolve its indeterminacy equivariantly:

\[
\begin{tikzcd}
\widetilde X \arrow[d,"\pi"'] \arrow[r,"\widetilde f"] &Y\\
X
\end{tikzcd}
\]

with \(\pi\) a sequence of blowups along smooth \(G\)-stable centers.

The line \(L_\sigma\) lifts through this sequence to an irreducible subvariety

\[
\widetilde L_\sigma\subset \widetilde X^\sigma
\]

which remains:

- \(N\)-stable;
- pointwise fixed by \(\sigma\);
- rationally chain connected.

Indeed, at each blowup:

- when the current carrier is not contained in the center, take its strict transform;
- when it is contained in the center, take the projectivization of a nonzero \(\sigma\)-eigenbundle in the normal bundle.

The latter is a projective bundle over the previous carrier, and \(N\) preserves its \(\sigma\)-eigenbundles because \(N\) centralizes \(\sigma\).

Equivariance now forces

\[
\widetilde f(\widetilde L_\sigma)\subset Y^\sigma.
\]

The image of a rationally chain connected variety is rationally chain connected. But the only positive-dimensional component of \(Y^\sigma\) is the elliptic curve \(C_\sigma\), which contains no rational curve. Therefore

\[
\widetilde f(\widetilde L_\sigma)=\{y\}
\]

is a single point.

Since \(\widetilde L_\sigma\) is \(N\)-stable and \(\widetilde f\) is equivariant,

\[
n\cdot y
=n\cdot\widetilde f(\widetilde L_\sigma)
=\widetilde f(n\cdot\widetilde L_\sigma)
=\widetilde f(\widetilde L_\sigma)
=y
\]

for every \(n\in N\). Hence \(y\in Y^N\), contradicting

\[
Y^N=\varnothing.
\]

This proves the claim.

The same argument also excludes an \(\alpha\)-equivariant map for any automorphism \(\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)\), since \(\alpha\) carries involutions and their centralizers to involutions and their centralizers.

## The reverse direction

For

\[
Y\dashrightarrow X,
\]

the argument fails in exactly the expected asymmetric way: the target fixed locus is

\[
X^\sigma=E_\sigma\sqcup L_\sigma,
\qquad
L_\sigma\simeq\mathbf P^1.
\]
