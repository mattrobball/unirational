# Picard group, intersections, and the two-ray game

Let \(\pi:Y=\operatorname{Bl}_C(X)\to X\), with \((g(C),\deg C)=(1,3)\).
Put \(H=\pi^*\mathcal O_X(1)\), denote the exceptional divisor by \(E\), and
put \(L=H-E\).  The pencil gives \(L=f^*\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^1}(1)\).

## Intersection ledger

For a curve blowup in a cubic threefold,

\[
 H^3=3,\quad H^2E=0,\quad HE^2=-\deg C=-3,
 \quad E^3=-\deg N_{C/X}=-6.
\]

Consequently

\[
 (-K_Y)^3=(2H-E)^3=12,
 \qquad L^2H=L^2E=L^3=0.
\]

The canonical and fibre classes are

\[
 K_Y=-2H+E,\qquad -K_Y=2H-E=H+L,\qquad [\text{fibre}]=L.
\]

## Curve rays

Over an algebraic closure choose:

- \(q\), a fibre of \(E=\mathbf P_C(N_{C/X})\to C\); it is contracted by
  \(\pi\) and maps isomorphically to the pencil base under \(f\);
- \(\ell\), a line on a smooth cubic-surface fibre of \(f\).

Their intersections are

| curve | \(H\) | \(E\) | \(L=H-E\) | \(-K_Y\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(q\) | 0 | -1 | 1 | 1 |
| \(\ell\) | 1 | 1 | 0 | 1 |

Thus

\[
 \overline{NE}(Y)=\mathbf R_{\ge0}[q]+\mathbf R_{\ge0}[\ell],
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Nef}(Y)=\mathbf R_{\ge0}[H]+\mathbf R_{\ge0}[L].
\]

The first ray gives \(\pi\); the second gives \(f\).  There is no flop.
Since \(-K_Y=H+L\) is positive on both rays (equivalently, it is the pullback
of an ample class through the graph embedding), \(Y\) is Fano.

## Effective and movable cones

The ideal of \(C\) in \(X\) is generated by the two degree-one equations
\(a_3,a_4\).  A strict transform of degree \(a\) with multiplicity \(b\ge0\)
along \(C\) has class \(aH-bE\), and effectiveness forces \(a\ge b\).
After separating fixed exceptional components this gives

\[
 \operatorname{Eff}(Y)=\mathbf R_{\ge0}[E]+\mathbf R_{\ge0}[L].
\]

The classes \(H\) and \(L\) are basepoint free.  Positive exceptional
coefficient is fixed, while every \(aH-bE\) with \(0\le b\le a\) is a
nonnegative combination of \(H\) and \(L\).  Hence

\[
 \operatorname{Mov}(Y)=\operatorname{Nef}(Y)
 =\mathbf R_{\ge0}[H]+\mathbf R_{\ge0}[L].
\]

All divisor classes and contractions descend over \(K_0\), because the frame,
plane, center, and pencil do.
