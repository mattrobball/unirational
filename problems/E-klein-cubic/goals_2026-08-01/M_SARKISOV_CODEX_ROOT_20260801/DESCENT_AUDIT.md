# Descent audit

## Why the constructed link descends

Let \(L=\mathbf C(W)\) and \(K_0=L^G\). Each of the five columns
\(x,C,D,E,K\) is an equivariant rational map \(W\dashrightarrow W\).
Evaluated at the generic point, it is therefore an invariant vector in the
twist of \(W_L\). The exact determinant witness proves that the five vectors
form a basis. Thus the coordinate equations \(a_i\), the plane
\(\Pi=(a_3,a_4)\), its intersection \(C\) with the twist, and the pencil
\([a_3:a_4]\) are defined over \(K_0\).

This is stronger than constructing the link over \(L\) and hoping it descends:
every equation in the graph model is already a \(K_0\)-equation. The
extraction is the ordinary blowup of a smooth \(K_0\)-subscheme, so the entire
two-ray game commutes with scalar extension.

## Why split rigidity does not descend automatically

A birational map from the generic twist to another Mori fibre space becomes,
after extension to \(L\), a semilinear \(G\)-equivariant map. Its coefficients
vary with the generic torsor parameter. It is not automatically one fixed
complex \(G\)-equivariant birational map, so split complex
\(G\)-birational-superrigidity theorems do not exclude it.

Equivalently, a center on the twist untwists to a \(G\)-equivariant rational
map from the generic torsor into a Hilbert scheme of centers, not necessarily
to a constant \(G\)-fixed Hilbert point. Any negative generic-twist theorem
would have to control those nonconstant semilinear centers.

## Dominance boundary

Birational rigidity controls birational maps between Mori fibre spaces.
Problem E asks for a dominant map from a representation, which can have
positive relative dimension or degree greater than one. No negative headline
conclusion follows from rigidity without a separate compression theorem.
This packet makes no such inference.
