# The genuine generic Jacobian and the degree-two/three Hilbert components

## 1. Setup and statement

Let

\[
G=\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbf F_{11}),\qquad
X=\{x_0^2x_1+x_1^2x_2+x_2^2x_3+x_3^2x_4+x_4^2x_0=0\}
   \subset\mathbf P(W),
\]

and let \(E=\mathbf C(\mathbf P(W))\),
\(K=E^G=K_{\rm proj}\).  The generic point of the free locus gives the
genuine projective generic \(G\)-torsor \(T=\operatorname{Spec}E\) over
\(K\).  Write \(X_T={}^T X\) and \(J_T={}^T J(X)\).

### Theorem R.1

\[
J_T(K)=\{0\}.
\]

### Theorem R.2

The threefold \(X_T\) has no geometrically integral \(K\)-defined conic.

### Theorem R.3

Let \(\overline{\mathcal T}\) be the closure of the smooth twisted-cubic
locus in the Hilbert scheme of \(X\), and let \(M_X\) be the smooth
fourfold of Bayer--Beentjes--Feyzbakhsh--Hein--Martinelli--Rezaee--Schmidt.
There are Aut\((X)\)-equivariant maps

\[
\overline{\mathcal T}\longrightarrow M_X
   \longrightarrow\Theta\subset J(X),
\]

where \(M_X\to\Theta\) is the blowup of the unique singular point
\(0\in\Theta\), and its exceptional divisor is canonically \(X\).  After
twisting,

\[
{}^T M_X(K)={}^T X(K)
\]

via the exceptional fibre.  In particular,

\[
{}^T\overline{\mathcal T}(K)\ne\varnothing
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
X_T(K)\ne\varnothing.
\]

The last implication includes boundary Hilbert points.  For a smooth
twisted cubic with normalization \(\mathbf P^1_K\), the implication is of
course already immediate by evaluating any \(K\)-point of the
normalization.

## 2. Rational maps from the torsor source to an abelian variety

A \(K\)-point of \(J_T\) is, by twisting adjunction, a
\(G\)-equivariant rational map

\[
f:\mathbf P(W)\dashrightarrow J(X).
\]

Every rational map from projective space over \(\mathbf C\) to an abelian
variety is constant.  One proof resolves the indeterminacy and observes
that every rational curve is contracted by a morphism to an abelian
variety; projective space is rationally chain connected.  Therefore

\[
J_T(K)=J(X)(\mathbf C)^G.
\]

It remains to compute the finite fixed group on the right.

## 3. Exact period-lattice calculation

Roulleau computes the period lattice \(\Lambda=H_1(J(X),\mathbf Z)\).
Put

\[
\nu=\frac{-1+\sqrt{-11}}2,\qquad
\mathcal O=\mathbf Z[\nu],\qquad
\delta=1+2\nu.
\]

In period vectors \(v_0,\ldots,v_4\), his theorem gives the
\(\mathcal O\)-basis

\[
\frac{v_0-3v_1+3v_2-v_3}{\delta},\quad
\frac{v_1-3v_2+3v_3-v_4}{\delta},\quad
v_0,\quad v_1,\quad v_2.
\]

Let \(\tau\) and \(\sigma\) be the order-11 and order-5 automorphisms in
the standard subgroup \(C_{11}\rtimes C_5<G\).  On the period vectors,

\[
\tau(v_k)=v_{k+1},\qquad
\sigma(v_k)=v_{5k},\qquad
\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1}=\tau^5.
\]

The producer reconstructs the two integral \(10\times10\) matrices on
\(\Lambda\).  It obtains

\[
\operatorname{SNF}(\tau-1)=
\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1,1,1,1,1,1,1,11).
\]

Since \(1\) is not an analytic eigenvalue of \(\tau\),

\[
J(X)^\tau\simeq\Lambda/(\tau-1)\Lambda\simeq\mathbf Z/11.
\]

Under this identification the normalizer action is not simply the action
of \(\sigma\) on the lattice quotient.  If
\(q=(\tau-1)z\), then

\[
(\tau-1)\sigma z
=\sigma(\tau^9-1)z
=\sigma(1+\tau+\cdots+\tau^8)q.
\]

The exact integral calculation shows that this endomorphism is
multiplication by \(3\pmod {11}\).  Because \(3\) has order five in
\(\mathbf F_{11}^{\times}\), its fixed subgroup is zero.  Hence

\[
J(X)^{\langle\tau,\sigma\rangle}=0,
\qquad J(X)^G=0,
\]

proving Theorem R.1.

## 4. All conics are closed by the residual-line theorem

Let \(C\subset X_T\) be a geometrically integral conic defined over
\(K\).  Every conic in projective space spans a plane, and formation of
the linear span commutes with base change, so
\(\Pi=\langle C\rangle\simeq\mathbf P^2_K\).  In the plane, the ideal of
\(C\) is generated by a quadratic form \(q\).  The cubic equation of
\(X_T\cap\Pi\) vanishes on \(C\), hence equals \(q\ell\) for a
\(K\)-linear form \(\ell\).  Thus the residual component is a
\(K\)-line \(L=V(\ell)\subset X_T\).

The binding repository theorem says that the genuine generic twist has no
\(K\)-line: a line would give a rational \(G\)-equivariant map from the
generic torsor source to the Fano surface, whose faithful image has
dimension at most two, contradicting \(\operatorname{ed}(G)\ge3\).
Therefore \(C\) cannot exist.  This proves Theorem R.2 and corrects the
phrase “non-plane conics” in the goal: there are none in projective
geometry.

## 5. Twisted cubics and the theta singularity

For any smooth complex cubic threefold, the Abel--Jacobi map of the
closure of the twisted-cubic locus has image the theta divisor and generic
fibre \(\mathbf P^2\).  The modern moduli compactification gives a
surjective map

\[
\overline{\mathcal T}\to M_X,
\]

and an Abel--Jacobi morphism \(M_X\to\Theta\) which is the blowup at the
unique singular point \(0\).  Its exceptional divisor is \(X\).

These constructions use the universal family, Chern classes, and the
canonical class \(H^2\), so they commute with automorphisms of \(X\).
They may therefore be twisted by \(T\).  If
\(m\in{}^T M_X(K)\), its image is a point of \(J_T(K)\), hence is zero by
Theorem R.1.  The scheme-theoretic fibre over zero is the twisted
exceptional divisor \(X_T\).  Conversely every point of \(X_T(K)\) is a
point of that exceptional divisor.  This proves

\[
{}^T M_X(K)=X_T(K)
\]

and Theorem R.3.

## 6. Consequence and strict boundary

The degree-three Abel--Jacobi base has no nonzero descended parameter.
The only \(K\)-points in its canonical desingularization lie over the
theta singularity, and choosing one is exactly choosing a point of the
original generic twist.  Thus the twisted-cubic Hilbert component does
not create a lower-dimensional torsor problem independent of the
headline.

This is a structural exit, not a positive or negative headline.  Rational
quartics and higher curves remain possible.  In particular, the theorem
that a generic Abel--Jacobi fibre of rational quartics is birational to the
cubic is proved under a generic-cubic hypothesis; the Klein cubic has
automorphism group \(G\) and does not satisfy that hypothesis.  This
packet does not silently transfer that theorem to the special fibre.
