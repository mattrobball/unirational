# A5 weak versality gives a degree-11 cycle on the full Schur twist

\[

## Weak-versality lemma

Let \(F\supset\mathbf C\) be infinite and let \(T/F\) be any \(H\)-torsor.
Twist the rational map above by \(T\).  The twist of the three-dimensional
linear representation is simply a three-dimensional \(F\)-vector space
\(W_T\) by linear Galois descent.  Consequently

\[
{}^T\mathbf P(V_3)=\mathbf P(W_T)\simeq\mathbf P^2_F.
\]

The domain of the twisted rational map is a nonempty open subset of this
projective plane.  Since \(F\) is infinite, \(\mathbf P^2(F)\) is Zariski
dense and meets that open.  Its image is an \(F\)-point on \({}^T X\).
Thus **every** \(H\)-twist of \(X\) is soluble over such a field.

## Application to the genuine full twist

Let \(E/K\) be the genuine generic \(G\)-torsor and choose either installed
maximal \(A_5\) subgroup \(H\).  Put \(L=E^H\).  Exact subgroup replay gives

\[
[L:K]=[G:H]=660/60=11.
\]

After base change to \(L\), the full Schur twist is the \(H\)-twist defined
by the \(H\)-torsor \(E/L\).  The lemma therefore gives a point in
\(X_E(L)\).  Pushing it down to \(K\) gives an effective zero-cycle of
degree 11.  Since 11 is prime, the field generated by its coordinates has
degree either 1 or 11 over \(K\).  Hence the precise dichotomy is:

```text
the full Schur twist has a K-point, or it has a degree-11 closed point.
```

This is a new theorem-level entry in the zero-cycle ledger.  It is stronger
than the previous subgroup-only status, because it applies the subgroup map
to the restriction of the actual full generic torsor.
