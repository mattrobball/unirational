# J1 — equivariant fixed-component blowup formula

## Theorem

Let \(Y\) be smooth projective over \(\mathbf C\), let \(t\) be an
involution, and let \(C\subset Y\) be a smooth \(t\)-stable centre.  Put
\(\widetilde Y=\operatorname{Bl}_C(Y)\).  For every connected component
\(D\subset C^t\), decompose the normal bundle into eigenbundles

\[
N_{C/Y}|_D=N_D^+\oplus N_D^-.
\]

The fixed part of the exceptional divisor over \(D\) is

\[
\mathbf P(N_D^+)\ \sqcup\ \mathbf P(N_D^-)
\]

after omitting a summand of rank zero and making the standard identification
of an eigenbundle projectivization with the exceptional locus inside the
strict transform of a fixed component when appropriate.  Away from fixed
components contained in the centre, the remaining fixed pieces are their
strict transforms; a component contained in the centre is represented by the
appropriate exceptional eigenbundle instead.

Every displayed projective bundle has

\[
\operatorname{Alb}(\mathbf P(N_D^\pm))\simeq\operatorname{Alb}(D),
\qquad
\operatorname{Pic}^0(\mathbf P(N_D^\pm))\simeq\operatorname{Pic}^0(D).
\]

These isomorphisms commute with every element of the normalizer that preserves
\(D\) and the chosen eigenbundle.  If a normalizer element exchanges the two
eigenbundles, it exchanges the two projective-bundle components and the
corresponding factors are induced rather than individually fixed.

## Proof

The assertion is local along \(D\).  In a \(t\)-linearized tubular chart,
the blowup exceptional divisor is \(\mathbf P(N_{C/Y})\).  A point of this
projective bundle is fixed precisely when its normal line is a
\(t\)-eigenline, hence it lies in one of the two projectivized eigenbundles.
The proper transforms account for points away from the exceptional divisor.
Projective-bundle invariance of \(\operatorname{Pic}^0\) and
\(\operatorname{Alb}\) proves the last display.  All constructions use the
linearized normal bundle and are therefore normalizer-functorial.  The same
argument applied componentwise handles a disconnected smooth invariant
centre.  \(\square\)

## One-motive form

For a fixed component \(F\) with marked boundary divisor \(D_F\), use the
Deligne-type two-term object

\[
\mathcal M(F,D_F)=
[\operatorname{Div}^0(D_F)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Pic}^0(F)].
\]

Under the blowup:

- a strict transform carries the pullback one-motive;
- a new fixed exceptional component over \(D\) carries the one-motive of
  \(D\), plus the lattice generated by its marked exceptional sections;
- incidence maps are pullback on divisors and norm/pushforward on Picard
  varieties;
- exchanged centres contribute induced pairs, not a pointwise-fixed factor.

This is the exact correction needed for pairs of centres exchanged by an
involution: if \(C\) and \(tC\) are disjoint, their exceptional divisors are
exchanged and add no component to \(\widetilde Y^t\), although their global
cohomology contributes a diagonal invariant subspace.

## Cohomological contribution

For a smooth centre of codimension \(c\),

\[
H^k(\operatorname{Bl}_C Y,\mathbf Q)
\simeq H^k(Y,\mathbf Q)\oplus
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{c-1}H^{k-2i}(C,\mathbf Q)(-i).
\]

In a fourfold and for \(k=3\), a curve centre contributes exactly
\(H^1(C)(-1)\).  A surface centre contributes the same \(H^1(C)(-1)\)
term.  Point and rational linear centres contribute no \(H^3\).  The formula
is equivariant; a disjoint orbit with stabilizer \(H\) contributes the
induced \(G\)-Hodge structure from \(H\).

## First-component propagation corollary

Consider a tower of smooth equivariant blowups starting at \(\mathbf P^4\),
and a fixed component \(F\) of the final space carrying a nonconstant
morphism \(F\to E_t\).  Trace \(F\) backward through the tower.  If it
descended through strict transforms all the way to an original linear fixed
component, its Albanese would remain zero, which is impossible.  At the first
stage where its ancestry is exceptional, it arises over a fixed part \(D\)
of the centre and

\[
\operatorname{Alb}(F)\simeq\operatorname{Alb}(D)
\longrightarrow E_t
\]

is nonzero.  Hence \(D\) has positive irregularity and its Albanese has
\(E_t\) as a quotient up to isogeny.  For a curve \(D\), this says that the
first such centre has positive genus.  For a surface centre, the correct
condition is \(q(D)>0\); excluding irregular surfaces would be an unjustified
strengthening.

If the component and map are residual-normalizer equivariant, the induced map
of Albanese torsors carries the affine class of `ONE_MOTIVE.md`.  When the
transition geometry additionally produces an invariant norm of degree \(d\)
over a base with trivial residual \(C_3\)-action, `ONE_MOTIVE.md` gives
\(3\mid d\).  No divisibility is asserted for a source that already carries
the matching affine class.

The proof is induction on the blowup tower.  Blowups of an old component do
not change its Albanese.  Every genuinely new fixed component is a projective
bundle over a fixed part of the centre, so its Albanese is that of the base.
A nonconstant morphism to an elliptic curve induces a nonzero homomorphism on
Albanese varieties.  Normalizer equivariance gives the torsor statement.

## Boundary

This proves J1, including normal eigenspaces, centre contributions, and
exchanged pairs.  It is a **conditional** propagation theorem, not an
exclusion theorem.  A dominant equivariant morphism need not induce a
surjection on fixed loci, so it need not provide any fixed component mapping
nonconstantly to \(E_t\).  Even when such a component exists,
positive-irregularity centres are legal, and the stabilizations in
`CENTRE_REALIZABILITY.md` construct them.  The overall headline remains OPEN.
