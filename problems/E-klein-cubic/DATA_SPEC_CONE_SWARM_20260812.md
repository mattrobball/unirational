## 0. The method (shared, and the one counterintuitive rule)

At a fixed degree `d`, the sealed window cell is a linear space of
candidate tuples (`d = 35`: dim 39, cut to 37 by the six universal flip
conditions — bases in `goal_runs_20260811/PAIR_ATTACK_D35/results/`,
`layer0_null_p*.npy` and `universal_matrix_6x39` in
`worked_example_p*.json`). The LANDING CONE is
`V = {c : F(T_c(x)) ≡ 0 in x}`, a cone; each sample point `x` gives one
CUBIC in the cell parameters.

* **Bounding rule.** For a generic `m`-dimensional section `L`,
  `dim(V ∩ L) = max(0, dim V + m − N)` where `N` = cell dimension. So
  `V ∩ L = {0}` proves `dim V ≤ N − m`.
* **Free rungs.** If the restricted cubics span ALL of `Sym³(L)` then
  every `tᵢ³` is in the ideal and `V ∩ L = {0}` with no solving. This
  works while `C(m+2,3) ≤` (global cubic span). At `d = 35` that gave
  `m = 19`, hence `dim V ≤ 18`, free.
* **Zero-dimensionality test.** Run `msolve -g 1` (leading ideal) and
  check that EVERY variable has a pure power among the leading
  monomials. That is equivalent to zero-dimensional, hence (the ideal
  being homogeneous) `V ∩ L = {0}`.
* **THE COUNTERINTUITIVE RULE — obey it.** Use the FULL span of landing
  cubics as generators, never a small subset. Correctness allows a
  subset (`V(subset) ⊇ V`), but COST does not: for `m` cubics in `n`
  variables the degree of regularity falls as `m` rises. Measured at
  `d = 35`, `n = 37`: 55 generators → degree 21 (unreachable: 10¹⁵
  columns); 520 → degree 7 (32M columns — this is why the sealed attempt
  walled); 1380 (full span) → **degree 5, 749 398 columns**. Always
  generate ≥ 1.4× the span in sample points, reduce to an independent
  basis, and feed all of it. Also pass `-t <threads>`.
