## 1. The socle sandwich

The five partials of a smooth cubic in five variables form a regular sequence
of quadrics, so the Jacobian ring `S/(F_0,...,F_4)` is Artinian Gorenstein with
Hilbert function `(1,5,10,10,5,1)` — socle degree `5`, verified symbolically in
`forced_foliation_witness.m2` for both the witness cubic and the Klein cubic.
Hence `(F_0,...,F_4)_m = S_m` for every `m >= 6`, so

```
m_y^6 ⊂ (F_0,...,F_4) ⊂ m_y^2                                       (19a)
```

(the right inclusion because the `F_i` are quadrics; the left because `m_y^6` is
generated in degree `6`).

> **Lemma 1.1 (the source's (19)).** `I_T^6 ⊂ I_Q ⊂ I_T^2`.

*Proof.* Substitution `psi : C[y] -> C[x]`, `y_i |-> T_i`, is a ring map with
`psi(m_y)·C[x] = I_T` and `psi((F_0,...,F_4))·C[x] = I_Q`. A ring map carries
`a ⊂ b` to `psi(a)C[x] ⊂ psi(b)C[x]`, and `psi(m_y^6)C[x] = I_T^6`,
`psi(m_y^2)C[x] = I_T^2` because `psi(a·b)C[x] = (psi(a)C[x])(psi(b)C[x])` for
ideals generated by the images. Apply this to (19a). ∎

> **Corollary 1.2 (the source's (20)).** `sqrt(I_Q) = sqrt(I_T)`.

So the degeneracy scheme of the pulled-back gradient is supported on exactly the
ambient base locus and introduces no new support away from it. This is what
makes the localisation argument of `DEFECT_SMITH_CLASSIFICATION.md` §2 work:
at the generic point of a **nonbase** divisor, `I_Q` is the unit ideal.

## 2. The maximal-minor product

> **Lemma 2.1 (the source's (21)).** `I_4(J_T) = I_P I_Q`, where
> `I_P = (P_0,...,P_4)`.

*Proof.* The entries of `adj(J_T)` are exactly the signed `4x4` minors of
`J_T`, so `I_4(J_T)` is the ideal generated by the entries of `adj(J_T)`. By
`adj(J_T) = P_T Q_T^t` those entries are the products `P_i F_j(T)`, whose ideal
is `I_P I_Q`. ∎

This is strictly stronger than "`P_T` spans the generic kernel": it identifies
the whole determinantal ideal. Two consequences used later:

* localised at the generic point of a nonbase divisor `D`, `I_Q` is the unit
  ideal (Corollary 1.2), so `I_4(J_T)_D = (a_T)_D` — the ambient rank-drop
  scheme has **divisorial part exactly `div(a_T)`**, `a_T = gcd(P_T)`;
* `I_4(J_T)` factors, which is a genuine restriction on the determinantal
  geometry: a `5x5` matrix of forms of degree `d-1` has no reason for its
  maximal-minor ideal to be a product of two ideals of five elements each.

## 3. Rank at most two at a base point

> **Lemma 3.1 (the source's (22)).** If `T(x_0) = 0` then
> `rank J_T(x_0) <= 2`.

*Proof.* Expand `T` at `x_0`: `T(x_0 + s u) = s J_T(x_0) u + O(s^2)`, since
`T(x_0) = 0`. Then `0 = F(T(x_0+su)) = s^3 F(J_T(x_0)u) + O(s^4)`, and the
coefficient of `s^3` gives `F(J u) = 0` for every `u`. So `F` vanishes on the
linear subspace `V = im J_T(x_0)`, i.e. `P(V) ⊂ X` is a linear subspace of
dimension `rank - 1`.

A smooth cubic threefold in `P^4` contains no plane. *Proof of that, for
completeness rather than by citation:* let `Lambda = {y_3 = y_4 = 0}` be a
plane with `Lambda ⊂ X`. Then `F in (y_3,y_4)`, say `F = y_3 A + y_4 B` with
`A,B` quadrics. On `Lambda`, `dF/dy_i = 0` automatically for `i <= 2`, while
`dF/dy_3 = A|_Lambda` and `dF/dy_4 = B|_Lambda`. So `Sing(X) ∩ Lambda` is the
intersection of two conics in `Lambda ≅ P^2`, which is nonempty by dimension.
Hence `X` is singular. (The same computation with `P^3 ⊂ X` is worse still.)

Therefore `rank - 1 <= 1`, i.e. `rank J_T(x_0) <= 2`. ∎

So the pointwise stratification is exhaustive and correct:

| stratum | condition | Jacobian |
|---|---|---|
| regular foliation locus | `I_T != 0`, `P_T != 0` | `rank J_T = 4` |
| nonbase critical locus | `I_T != 0`, `P_T = 0` | `rank J_T <= 3` |
| base locus | `I_T = 0` | `rank J_T <= 2` |

Rank `2` is line-type, rank `1` point-type, rank `0` a higher-multiplicity base
stratum. This recovers the local line/conic/higher-cluster alternatives of
`SLICE_CLASSIFICATION.md` **without** forcing a line normal form — which is the
right conclusion, since `REFUTATION_POINTED_CURVE_EXCLUSION.md` refuted the
generic-line normal form (`GENERIC-COMMON-FACTOR-LINE-NORMAL-FORM-REFUTED`).

## 4. The Klein–Nambu wedge identities

Let `Omega = dx_0 ^ ... ^ dx_4`, `omega_T = iota_{P_T} Omega`, and
`eps_m = dx_0 ^ ... ^ \hat{dx_m} ^ ... ^ dx_4`, so
`iota_P Omega = sum_m (-1)^m P_m eps_m`.

> **Lemma 4.1 (the source's (23)).** For each `i`,
> ```
> /\_{j != i} dT_j  =  (-1)^i F_i(T) · omega_T                       (23)
> ```
> (wedge taken in increasing order of `j`).

*Proof.* The coefficient of `eps_m` in `/\_{j!=i} dT_j` is the `4x4` minor of
`J_T` on rows `{j != i}` and columns `{l != m}`, i.e. `M_{im}`, and
`adj(J_T)_{mi} = (-1)^{m+i} M_{im}`. Hence

```
/\_{j!=i} dT_j = sum_m M_{im} eps_m = sum_m (-1)^{m+i} adj(J_T)_{mi} eps_m
               = (-1)^i sum_m (-1)^m P_m F_i(T) eps_m
               = (-1)^i F_i(T) iota_{P_T} Omega. ∎
```

So the five maximal differential wedges have the single common factor
`omega_T`, and their cofactors are exactly the pulled-back partials of the
Klein cubic. Together with `d(iota_P Omega) = div(P) · Omega` and
`THEOREM_FORCED_FOLIATION.md` (12), `d omega_T = 0`. Every landing tuple thus
carries a **Klein–Nambu package** `(T, Q_T, P_T, omega_T)` with

```
F(T) = 0,  Q_T^t J_T = 0,  adj(J_T) = P_T Q_T^t,  J_T P_T = 0,  d omega_T = 0.
```

The source's closing remark is correct and worth keeping: a general
divergence-free covariant gives no such package; it must in addition possess
four algebraically independent first integrals whose unique algebraic relation
is `F = 0`.

## 5. Saturation: `a_T` is an invariant, and a Darboux factor

Write `a_T = gcd(P_0,...,P_4)`, `P_T = a_T R_T`, `e = deg a_T`,
`m = deg R_T = 2d-4-e`.

**(a) `a_T` is an invariant.** `P_T(gx) = g P_T(x)` gives
`a_T(gx) = chi(g) a_T(x)` for a character `chi` of `G`; `G = PSL(2,11)` is
perfect, so `chi = 1` and `a_T in S^G`. (The source's (25).)

**(b) `F` does not divide `a_T`.** `a_T | Delta_T`, and
`Delta_T|_X != 0` because the restricted selfmap is dominant
(`THEOREM_SOURCE_TANGENCY.md` (T6) and §5). So `F` divides neither.

**(c) `e = 0` or `e >= 5`.** Two independent routes, both exact:
`I(1) = I(2) = I(4) = 0` and `I(3) = 1` (spanned by `F`), so an invariant of
degree `<= 4` is either constant or a multiple of `F`; the latter is excluded
by (b). Alternatively, restrict to `X`: `H^0(X,O_X(e))^G = 0` for
`1 <= e <= 4`. Both tables are recomputed in `verify_d35_dimensions.py`.
(The source's (26).)

**(d) The saturated foliation is `O(5-2d+e) -> T_{P^4}`,** the source's (27),
i.e. `O(1-m) -> T_{P^4}` with `m = 2d-4-e`. This is the correction already
recorded as `THEOREM_FORCED_FOLIATION.md` §3 and `FOLIATION_REFORMULATION.md`
(F4): the *unsaturated* line bundle is `O(5-2d)`, and the saturation is lossy —
in the packet's exact witness `deg P_T = 10` but `e = 8`, so `m = 2`.

**(e) Darboux (the source's (28)).** From `div(P_T) = 0` and `P_T = a_T R_T`,

```
R_T(a_T) + a_T div(R_T) = 0.                                        (28)
```

If `h` is an irreducible factor of `a_T` with multiplicity `s >= 1`, write
`a_T = h^s u`, `h ∤ u`. Substituting into (28) and dividing by `h^{s-1}` gives
`s u R_T(h) + h(...) = 0`, so `h | s u R_T(h)`; since `h ∤ u` and `s != 0` in
characteristic zero, `h | R_T(h)`. So `V(h)` is invariant under the saturated
field: **every irreducible component of the ambient critical divisor is a
Darboux hypersurface of the saturated foliation**, and by (a) the whole
arrangement is `G`-invariant of degree in `{0} ∪ {5,6,...}`.

## 6. The singular scheme of the saturated foliation

The saturated field is a section of `T_{P^4}(m-1)`, and its zero scheme is the
locus where `R_T(x)` is radial.

> **Lemma 6.1 (the source's (29)).**
> `int_{P^4} c_4(T_{P^4}(m-1)) = m^4 + m^3 + m^2 + m + 1.`

*Proof.* `c_i(T_{P^4}) = binom(5,i) h^i`, so for a rank-`4` twist
`c_4(T(a)) = sum_{i=0}^{4} binom(5,i) a^{4-i}`. With `a = m-1` and `u = m-1`
this is `u^4+5u^3+10u^2+10u+5 = ((u+1)^5 - 1)/u = (m^5-1)/(m-1)`. ∎

Since that number is `>= 1` for every `m >= 0`, a nowhere-vanishing section is
impossible:

> **Corollary 6.2 (the source's (30)).** The saturated foliation is **never**
> nonsingular. When its singular scheme is zero-dimensional its length is
> exactly `m^4+m^3+m^2+m+1`.

At `d = 35` with `a_T = 1` (so `m = 66`) that length is

```
66^4 + 66^3 + 66^2 + 66 + 1 = 19 266 655.                            (31)
```

Recomputed exactly. It is a large forced global singular scheme and **not** a
contradiction; recorded as a numerical fact, not an obstruction.

## 7. Non-claims

* Everything here is a **necessary** condition on a landing tuple; nothing is
  excluded.
* (28) constrains the ambient critical divisor but does not bound its degree
  from above; `e` is only known to lie in `{0} ∪ {5,...,2d-5}`.
* (31) counts a scheme whose dimension is not known to be zero; if the singular
  scheme has positive-dimensional components the number is a localised
  contribution, not a length.
* Nothing here uses `G`-equivariance except in 5(a), 5(c) and the invariance of
  the Darboux arrangement.
