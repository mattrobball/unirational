## Executive result

Assume that a primitive homogeneous ambient landing tuple

\[
T=Hx+FQ,\qquad F(T)=0,\qquad [T]|_X=\operatorname{id}_X
\]

is a rational `G`-retraction.  Put

\[
B=V_X(H).
\]

The exact polar identities imply that, at a general point of `B`, the line
`<x,Q(x)>` lies on `X`.  The associated line map is defined by the canonical,
gauge-independent Pluecker tuple

\[
\Pi_{ij}=\frac{x_iT_j-x_jT_i}{F}=x_iQ_j-x_jQ_i.
\]

Let `J_line` be the ideal generated by these minors on `B`, and let

\[
\Gamma_{\rm line}
=\operatorname{Proj}_B\overline{\mathcal R(J_{\rm line})}
\]

be its normalized graph.  This is selected by the actual landing tuple, not by
an arbitrary resolution.

For every involution `t`, define

\[
R_t=
\{(x,y)\in E_t\times L_t:\Phi(x,y,y)=0\}.
\]

Then:

1. `R_t` is the connected smooth genus-four curve parametrizing the mixed
   `t`-fixed lines on `X`; `R_t -> E_t` has degree two and `R_t -> L_t`
   has degree three.
2. Every irreducible component of the normalized line graph above `E_t` which
   dominates `E_t` maps finitely and dominantly to `R_t`.
3. Consequently its degree over `E_t` is even.  In particular, a birational
   elliptic carrier over `E_t` is impossible in the retraction branch.
4. If such a component has degree `2k` over `E_t`, its normalization has genus
   at least `3k+1`; the first possible carrier has genus four.
5. The base divisor `B` is singular along every one of the 55 elliptics
   `E_t`.  This is forced directly by invariance: `H|_{E_t}=0` and `t=-1` on
   both normal directions.
6. If `B` is irreducible, the resolved line map `B -->> F(X)` is dominant.
   Hence every smooth resolution `Btilde` satisfies
   \[
   q(Btilde)\ge5,\qquad p_g(Btilde)\ge10,
   \qquad \rho(Btilde)\ge25.
   \]
7. The residual `S3`-module on `H^0(R_t,Omega^1)` is
   \[
   1\oplus\operatorname{sgn}\oplus\operatorname{std}.
   \]
   Its `G`-orbit carries the five-dimensional Weil representation.  By
   contrast, the orbit of the original elliptics `E_t` does not.  Thus the
   genus-four boundary curves are the first explicit carriers which can supply
   the Hodge representation required by a dominant ambient map.

## What changed

The degree-one branch no longer stops merely at an abstract section of the
six-line incidence cover.  The actual normalized Pluecker ideal selects a
specific all-involution carrier system, and its source degrees, target curves,
genera, residual characters, and global Hodge contribution are determined.

The relevant Fano curve is **not** Roulleau's genus-two component `D_t`.
Roulleau decomposes the incidence divisor through the fixed line as

\[
C_{L_t}=D_t+R_t,
\]

where `D_t` has genus two and the residual component `R_t` has genus four.
The retraction boundary contains the source point `x in E_t`, so it lands on
`R_t`, the curve of mixed fixed lines.

## Exact remaining gap

The theorem does not exclude the retraction.  It reduces it to the existence
of a `G`-invariant hypersurface section `B=V_X(H)` which

- is singular in codimension one along all 55 elliptics;
- has normalized Pluecker carriers giving finite covers of all 55 curves
  `R_t`;
- and, on every component carrying at least two involution elliptics, admits a
  dominant rational map to the Klein Fano surface.

The next exact target is to combine the conductor formula for the normalization
of `B` with the Pluecker Hurwitz formula and the known intersection lattice of
the 55 Fano curves.  A contradiction there would exclude the nonsquare
retraction branch.
