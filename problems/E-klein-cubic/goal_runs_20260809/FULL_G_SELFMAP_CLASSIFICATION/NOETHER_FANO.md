# Noether--Fano analysis

Let

\[
\mathcal M=\varphi^{-1}|H|\subset|nH|
\]

be the mobile system of a dominant \(G\)-equivariant rational selfmap. On a
smooth graph resolution,

\[
q^*H=np^*H-\sum_E m_EE.
\tag{1.1}
\]

## 1. Accepted canonicality

Full-\(G\) birational superrigidity gives canonicality of the scaled pair

\[
\left(X,\frac2n\mathcal M\right),
\]

hence

\[
2m_E\le n a_E
\tag{1.2}
\]

for every divisorial valuation \(E\) over \(X\).

This is a valuable bound, but it is not a degree formula.

## 2. Exact degree relation

The degree is

\[
3\delta=
\left(np^*H-\sum_E m_EE\right)^3.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Expanding (2.1) involves self-intersections and mixed intersections of the
exceptional divisors. Inequality (1.2) does not determine their signs or
values. In particular it does not imply \(3\delta=3n^3\), nor does it force
\(n=1\).

The tangent-residual theorem now supplies actual degree-\(>1\) equivariant
selfmaps. Therefore no argument using only canonicality of the scaled pair,
Picard rank one of \(X\), and the orbit census can prove degree one.

## 3. Maximal centres

For the mobile system of a full-\(G\) selfmap there is no maximal singularity
in the usual superrigidity sense: (1.2) holds. Any contradiction must use
stronger information than the ordinary Noether--Fano threshold.

The installed orbit types -- \(C_{11}\)-points, \(C_5\)-points, type-I and
type-II \(V_4\)-points, \(C_6\)-points, fixed lines, and fixed elliptics --
remain relevant for an **ambient landing ideal**, but the abstract multiplicity
inequality cannot eliminate them all. The section-selected tangent maps show
that a valid base ideal can hide its fixed behavior on exceptional geometry.

## 4. Corti and multiplicity-degree inequalities

Corti inequalities can exclude a proposed noncanonical centre. Here the
scaled pair is already canonical. Applying stronger local inequalities would
require additional lower bounds on multiplicities forced by the ambient
landing equation. The exact strata and normal characters provide possible
locations and characters, not those lower bounds.

The missing input is therefore not another enumeration of centres. It is an
ideal-theoretic statement such as:

\[
\text{landing identity}
\Longrightarrow
\text{a specified Rees valuation has multiplicity exceeding (1.2)}.
\]

No such implication is currently proved.

## 5. Consequence for the mission

A theorem of the form “every degree-\(>1\) selfmap has an impossible maximal
centre” is false for arbitrary selfmaps. It may still be true for the
ambient-extendable subclass, because that subclass satisfies the much stronger
polynomial identity \(F(P)=0\) and has forced plus-plane base components.
