# MMP and birational rigidity

## 1. What full-\(G\) superrigidity proves

For the Klein cubic with the full \(G\)-action, birational superrigidity
controls \(G\)-equivariant **birational** maps from \(X\) to \(G\)-Mori fibre
spaces. In particular,

\[
\operatorname{Bir}^G(X)=\operatorname{Aut}^G(X)=1.
\]

Hence every degree-one \(G\)-equivariant rational selfmap is the identity.
This is the exact and important degree-one conclusion.

## 2. Conditional finite-graph argument

Let

\[
Z\longrightarrow Y\xrightarrow{\nu}X
\]

be the Stein factorization of a smooth graph resolution, with \(Y\) normal
and finite of degree \(\delta\) over \(X\). If

1. \(Y\) is terminal;
2. \(Y\) is \(\mathbf Q\)-factorial;
3. \(-K_Y\) is ample; and
4. \(\rho(Y)^G=1\),

then \(Y\to\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf C\) is a \(G\)-Mori fibre space.
Superrigidity identifies the birational map \(X\dashrightarrow Y\) with an
isomorphism, and \(\nu\) becomes a regular endomorphism of \(X\). Beauville's
theorem then gives \(\delta=1\).

The logic is correct only with all four hypotheses proved for the actual
finite model.

## 3. Actual counterpressure from tangent-residual selfmaps

The tangent-residual construction gives an actual nonidentity
\(G\)-equivariant rational selfmap of degree at least three. Apply the
conditional argument to its normalized Stein model. At least one of the four
hypotheses must fail.

Thus the normalized-graph defect is not merely a theoretical loophole. It is
forced by actual maps. The existence theorem does not determine whether the
defect is:

- nonterminal or noncanonical singularities;
- failure of \(\mathbf Q\)-factoriality;
- failure of Fano ampleness;
- or invariant Picard rank greater than one.

## 4. Relative MMP over the target

Running a \(G\)-equivariant MMP on a resolution over the target contracts
\(q\)-vertical extremal rays. It can produce a relative minimal or canonical
model, but it does not turn a generically finite map into a birational map.
Horizontal ramification remains, and the output need not be a rank-one Fano
threefold over a point.

Moreover, finite normalization and relative MMP do not commute in a way that
preserves the source's birational identification with \(X\) together with a
finite morphism to the original target. A Sarkisov decomposition is a theorem
about birational maps between Mori fibre spaces, not about the finite arrow
\(Y\to X\).

## 5. MMP over the source

Running MMP relative to \(p:Z\to X\) simply recovers a birational model over
the source. It may contract exceptional divisors needed to resolve \(q\), so
\(q\) can cease to be a morphism. It gives no degree reduction.

## 6. Ramification pair

The formula

\[
K_Z=q^*K_X+R_q
\]

suggests treating \((Z,R_q)\) or an orbifold branch pair on \(X\). But
\(R_q\) is horizontal for a degree-greater-than-one map, and log-MMP of this
pair is compatible with the existence of finite covers of Fano varieties.
Without a coefficient or singularity theorem forced by the ambient landing
identity, the log viewpoint supplies no contradiction.

## 7. Klein-specific theorem that could still work

A valid ambient rigidity theorem would have to prove that the global landing
identity forces the finite Stein model into the four-hypothesis class, or
forces a specific non-Mori defect that contradicts the normalized Rees
carrier data. This would be special to ambient-extendable maps; it is false
for arbitrary selfmaps.

## 8. Boundary

There is no theorem of the form

\[
\text{full-\(G\) birational superrigidity}
\Longrightarrow
\text{all dominant rational selfmaps are birational}.
\]

The tangent-residual maps are explicit existential counterexamples to that
implication on the Klein cubic itself. Birational rigidity remains decisive
only after degree one has been obtained by some independent, ambient-specific
argument.
