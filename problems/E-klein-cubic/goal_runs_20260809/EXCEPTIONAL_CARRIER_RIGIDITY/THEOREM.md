# Normalized-Rees carrier theorem and exact remaining gap

## 1. Setup

Let
\[
g:X\dashrightarrow X
\]
be the dominant `G`-equivariant rational selfmap obtained by restricting a
hypothetical ambient landing covariant to the Klein cubic.  Let `J` be the
primitive base ideal sheaf of a homogeneous tuple defining `g`, after removing
any common divisorial factor on `X`.  Put
\[
\mathcal R(J)=\bigoplus_{m\geq0}J^m t^m,
\qquad
\Gamma=\operatorname{Proj}_X\overline{\mathcal R(J)}.
\]
Then `Gamma` is the normalization of the graph closure
\[
G_g\subset X\times X.
\]
Write
\[
\pi:\Gamma\to X,
\qquad q:\Gamma\to X
\]
for the source and target morphisms.  The `G`-stability of `J` gives a unique
`G`-action on `Gamma` making both maps equivariant.

All carrier statements below concern this `Gamma`.  A later principalization is
used only to compute on a smooth model; it does not define the carriers.

## 2. Joint-residue survival theorem

Let `v` be a divisorial valuation of `C(X)` whose center on `X` is an
irreducible subvariety `Z`.  Let `K_v` be the closure of the center of `v` on
`Gamma`.  Choose a target projective chart in which one coordinate `p_j` of the
landing tuple has minimal `v`-order.  In the residue field `kappa(v)` put
\[
L_v=
\mathbf C(Z)\bigl(
\overline{p_i/p_j}:i=0,\ldots,4
\bigr).
\tag{2.1}
\]
Zero residues are allowed.  The field is independent of the chosen minimal
coordinate.

### Theorem 2.1 — center dimension

There are inclusions
\[
L_v\subset \mathbf C(K_v)\subset\kappa(v),
\]
the first extension is finite, and
\[
\dim K_v=\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbf C}L_v.
\tag{2.2}
\]

If `K_v` is a divisor on the normal threefold `Gamma`, then
\[
\mathbf C(K_v)=\kappa(v).
\]
Conversely, when `v(J)>0`, `v` is a Rees valuation of `J` precisely when
`dim K_v=2`.

### Proof

Let `T_v` be the center of `v` on the nonnormal graph closure `G_g`.  On the
chosen target chart, the local coordinate ring of `G_g` is generated by source
functions and the ratios `p_i/p_j`.  Passing to residues at `v` shows
\[
\mathbf C(T_v)=L_v.
\]
The normalization `Gamma→G_g` is finite, so `K_v→T_v` is finite.  This gives
the finite field extension and the dimension equality.  If `K_v` has codimension
one, normality makes its generic local ring the valuation ring of `v`, hence its
function field is `kappa(v)`.  Exceptional prime divisors of the normalized
blowup are exactly the Rees valuations.  This proves the theorem.

## 3. Point-centered rigidity

Let `x` be a type-I or type-II `V4` point and let `v` be centered at `x`.
Then (2.1) contains no positive-dimensional source field, and therefore
\[
\dim K_v
=
\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbf C}
\mathbf C\bigl(\overline{p_i/p_j}\bigr).
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Corollary 3.1

A point-centered divisor survives on the normalized graph if and only if its
initial target image is two-dimensional.

In particular:

1. a point-centered divisor whose map lands in `L_z`, `E_z`, or any target
   curve is contracted;
2. no point-centered Rees divisor is fixed pointwise by a nontrivial
   involution;
3. every `V4`-stable point-centered Rees divisor has faithful generic `V4`
   action and maps generically finitely to a `V4`-stable surface in `X`.

For (2), an involution-fixed divisor would map into
`X^t=E_t\sqcup L_t`, contradicting (3.1).  For (3), the same argument
applied to each nonidentity element excludes a generic kernel.

This is the first genuine exceptional-carrier rigidity theorem in the packet.
It excludes line-valued **divisors** over a marked point, but not line-valued
curve components of the normalized point fiber and not fixed curve slices
inside a surface-valued divisor.

## 4. Canonical ordinary carrier over a fixed curve

Let `S` be `E_t` or `L_t`, and let `v_S` be the ordinary valuation obtained by
blowing up the smooth curve `S` in `X`.  Its residue field is
\[
\kappa(v_S)=\mathbf C(S)(z),
\]
the function field of `P(N_{S/X})`.  Let
\[
K_S:=K_{v_S}\subset\Gamma.
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — existence, uniqueness, and dimension

Assume `J` vanishes at the generic point of `S`.  Then:

1. `K_S` is a canonical irreducible carrier, unique on every common
   equivariant refinement;
2. `K_S` is stable under `N_G(<t>)/<t> ~= S3`;
3. `t` acts trivially on `K_S` and
   \[
   q(K_S)\subset E_t\sqcup L_t;
   \]
4. `q|_{K_S}` is nonconstant;
5. `dim K_S` is one or two;
6. if `dim K_S=1`, then `K_S→S` is birational;
7. if `dim K_S=2`, then `v_S` is a Rees valuation and `K_S` is birational to
   `P(N_{S/X})`; its nonconstant map to the fixed curve has a Stein
   factorization through a normal curve.

If `J` does not vanish generically on `S`, the strict transform of `S` in
`Gamma` is instead the canonical carrier and is birational to `S`.

### Proof

The valuation center gives (1).  The valuation is residual invariant, giving
(2).  An involution fixing a curve in a smooth threefold acts as `-1` on both
normal directions, hence trivially on their projectivization and on
`kappa(v_S)`; this proves (3).  A constant image would be a common fixed point
of the residual `S3` on `X^t`.  No such point exists: translation by `q_t` is
fixed-point-free on `E_t`, and the faithful dihedral action has no global fixed
point on `L_t`.  Thus (4).

The joint-residue theorem gives (5).  In the one-dimensional case,
`C(K_S)` is algebraic over `C(S)` and lies in `C(S)(z)`.  The field `C(S)` is
relatively algebraically closed in the purely transcendental extension, so
`C(K_S)=C(S)`, proving (6).  In the two-dimensional case the center is a prime
divisor for the ordinary valuation, hence has residue field `C(S)(z)`, proving
(7).

## 5. Integration of the accepted normal jet

The first nonzero ordinary normal term defines only an associated-graded class
at first.  Theorem 4.1 upgrades it as follows:
\[
\mathbf P(N_{S/X})
\dashrightarrow K_S
\xrightarrow{q}X.
\tag{5.1}
\]
The first arrow is the rational map induced by the inclusion
`C(K_S)⊂C(S)(z)`.  Thus the ordinary formal jet always integrates to the
actual morphism `q|K_S`; what can fail is survival of a particular prime in a
later weak base locus as a distinct Rees component.

For `S=E_t`, the accepted transition theorem says that the first nonzero
ordinary normal order is odd and lands in `L_t`.  Therefore the canonical
ordinary carrier over `E_t` is line-valued.  It is not an elliptic selfcarrier
and cannot be identified with the strict `[-5]` boundary map.

Any elliptic-target carrier, if one exists, is a secondary fixed component of
`Gamma` or a fixed slice in a higher-dimensional carrier.  Its existence and
numerical data are not supplied by the ordinary jet.

## 6. Mapwise finiteness and global coupling

For a fixed `J`, the following sets are finite:

- Rees valuations of `J`;
- irreducible components of every normalized point fiber;
- irreducible components of the fixed loci `Gamma^H`;
- irreducible components of the intersections used to define fixed carrier
  incidences.

The `G`-action transports these objects.  Thus all carrier dimensions, target
components, residue degrees, and base multiplicities are constant on complete
`G`-orbits.  Choices over the 55 involutions and 55 `V4` points are not
independent.

This proves a finite carrier profile **for each fixed hypothetical map**.  It
does not give a finite list uniform over all possible `G`-stable landing ideals.

## 7. Polarization boundary

Let `C` be a one-dimensional elliptic-target carrier dominating `E_t` with
source degree `delta_t` and elliptic multiplier `n`.  On a principalization,
\[
3n^2=3d\delta_t-B\cdot C.
\tag{7.1}
\]
The ordinary curve carrier has source degree one, but it is line-valued.  The
packet gives no theorem forcing a secondary elliptic carrier to have
`delta_t=1` or `B·C=0`.

Hence `n=-5` implies only
\[
75=3d\delta_t-B\cdot C.
\]
The implication `d=25` remains conditional on
\[
\delta_t=1,
\qquad B\cdot C=0.
\]

## 8. Verdict

The normalized-Rees language closes the definitional and formal-integration
gap, and the joint-residue theorem excludes all point-centered line-valued
Rees divisors.  It does not classify the curve components and fixed slices of
the actual type-I/type-II fibers.  The exact exit is therefore

```text
EXCEPTIONAL-CARRIER-INTEGRATION-UNDECIDED
```
