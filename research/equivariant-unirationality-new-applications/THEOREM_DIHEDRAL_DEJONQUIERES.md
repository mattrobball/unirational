# The odd-dihedral de Jonquières theorem

## Statement

Fix an odd integer `m>=3` and a primitive `m`th root of unity `zeta`. Let
`B=P^1_[X:Z]`, let

\[
\mathcal E=\mathcal O_B\oplus\mathcal O_B(m)\oplus\mathcal O_B(m),
\]

and use fiber coordinates `[W:U:V]`. Define

\[
S_m=\{UV=(X^{2m}+Z^{2m})W^2\}\subset\mathbf P_B(\mathcal E). \tag{1}
\]

Let `D_{2m}=<r,s | r^m=s^2=1, srs=r^{-1}>` act on the base by

\[
r[X:Z]=[\zeta X:Z],\qquad s[X:Z]=[Z:X],
\]

using the natural linearization on `O(m)`. Let `delta` exchange `U` and
`V`, and put

\[
G_m=\langle\delta\rangle\times D_{2m}.
\]

Then:

\[
\boxed{S_m\text{ is rational and satisfies Condition (A), but is not
weakly }G_m\text{-versal}.}
\]

In particular `S_m` is not `G_m`-unirational. Moreover the equivariant
universal-torsor obstruction and every higher Amitsur group vanish for the
action and for every subgroup of `G_m`.

The same group acting trivially on the first factor of

\[
X_m=\mathbf P^1\times S_m
\]

makes `X_m` a rational conic-bundle threefold that is not weakly
`G_m`-versal. This second assertion requires the residual-RCC theorem:
`X_m^delta=P^1 x C_m` contains rational curves.

## 1. Geometry of the conic bundle

Equation (1) has `2m` singular fibers, over the simple zeros of
`X^{2m}+Z^{2m}`. Each is the reduced union `UV=0` of two lines. The total
space is smooth: near a zero of the discriminant and in the chart `W=1`,
the equation is analytically `uv=t`; at the two points with `W=0`, one of
the partial derivatives with respect to `U,V` is nonzero.

The section

\[
[W:U:V]=[0:1:0]
\]

shows that the generic conic is split. Hence `S_m` is birational to a
`P^1`-bundle over `P^1` and is rational.

The involution `delta` has no negative-eigenline point on `S_m`; its fixed
locus is therefore

\[
C_m=S_m^\delta=\{U=V,\ U^2=(X^{2m}+Z^{2m})W^2\}. \tag{2}
\]

In the affine chart `Z=W=1`, this is

\[
y^2=x^{2m}+1.
\]

The branch polynomial has `2m` distinct roots, so `C_m` is smooth of genus
`m-1`.

## 2. Residual dihedral action on the fixed curve

On the affine model of `C_m`, the action is

\[
r(x,y)=(\zeta x,y),\qquad
s(x,y)=(x^{-1},x^{-m}y). \tag{3}
\]

The relation `srs=r^{-1}` is immediate, and (3) preserves
`y^2=x^{2m}+1`.

The full dihedral group has no fixed point on `C_m`. Indeed, a common fixed
point would project to a common fixed point of `D_{2m}` on `P^1`; a
nontrivial rotation fixes only `0` and `infinity`, while the reflection
interchanges those points.

Consequently

\[
S_m^{G_m}=C_m^{D_{2m}}=\varnothing. \tag{4}
\]

## 3. Condition (A)

Let `A<=G_m` be abelian. Since `delta` is central, it is enough to find a
fixed point for `<A,delta>`; hence we may assume `delta in A`. The
projection of `A` to `D_{2m}` is abelian. Because `m` is odd, every
abelian subgroup of `D_{2m}` is either contained in the rotation group or
is generated by one reflection.

- A rotation subgroup fixes the two points of `C_m` over `x=0`, namely
  `(0,+1)` and `(0,-1)`.
- A reflection `r^k s` fixes a point of the base satisfying
  `x^2=zeta^k`. Since `m` is odd, one of the two square roots can be chosen
  with `x^m=1`. At that point, the fixed-point condition on `y` is
  automatic and `y^2=2`, giving two fixed points on `C_m`.

Thus every abelian `A` fixes a point of `C_m subset S_m`, and Condition (A)
holds.

## 4. The fixed-stratum obstruction

Apply Corollary T3.1 of `theory/FIX_T_gate.md` with the central element
`delta`. The positive-dimensional fixed locus is the genus-`m-1` curve
`C_m`, so it contains no rational curve, and (4) gives the empty deeper
fixed locus. Therefore no faithful linear `G_m`-source admits an
equivariant rational map to `S_m`. The action is not weakly versal and not
`G_m`-unirational.

For `X_m=P^1 x S_m`, the central fixed locus is

\[
X_m^\delta=\mathbf P^1\times C_m.
\]

It contains all fibers `P^1 x {c}`, so the original central theorem does
not apply. Nevertheless, an irreducible RCC subvariety maps constantly to
`C_m`; if it were `G_m`-stable, its base point would be
`D_{2m}`-fixed, contradicting `C_m^{D_{2m}}=emptyset`. Theorem G1 applies,
proving the threefold assertion.

## 5. Cohomological obstruction audit

Every Sylow subgroup of `G_m` fixes a point:

- a 2-Sylow is generated by `delta` and a reflection and fixes one of the
  reflection points constructed above;
- a `p`-Sylow for odd `p|m` lies in the rotation group and fixes a point
  above `x=0`.

The same is true for Sylow subgroups of every subgroup of `G_m`. The
restriction-corestriction argument therefore kills the universal-torsor
obstruction for every subgroup. Since `Pic(S_m)` and `Pic(X_m)` are free
and finitely generated, all higher Amitsur groups vanish by
Scavia--Tschinkel--Zhang. For the Bogomolov multiplier, let `R=<r>`. It is cyclic and normal, and
`G_m/R` is the abelian group `C_2 x C_2`. Every subgroup `H<=G_m` has
cyclic normal subgroup `H intersect R` and abelian quotient. Bogomolov's
cyclic-by-abelian vanishing lemma therefore gives `B_0(H)=0` for every
subgroup.

Thus the negative theorem is not detected by Condition (A), ordinary or
higher Amitsur groups, the universal-torsor obstruction, or the Bogomolov
multiplier.

## 6. Smallest case

For `m=3`,

\[
G_3=C_2\times D_6\simeq C_2\times S_3,
\qquad C_3:\ y^2=x^6+1
\]

and `g(C_3)=2`. This is the recommended headline example.
