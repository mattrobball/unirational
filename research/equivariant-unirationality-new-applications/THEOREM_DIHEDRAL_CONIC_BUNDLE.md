# Theorem: odd-dihedral rational conic bundles are not weakly versal

## Statement

Fix an odd integer `n >= 3`.  Let

\[
H_n=D_{2n}=\langle r,s\mid r^n=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle
\]

be the dihedral group of order `2n`.  On `P^1_[x:y]` define

\[
r[x:y]=[\zeta_nx:y],\qquad s[x:y]=[y:x].
\]

Let

\[
F_n=x^{2n}+y^{2n}
\]

and define

\[
S_n=\{uv=F_n(x,y)w^2\}
 \subset \mathbf P_{\mathbf P^1}
 \bigl(\mathcal O(n)\oplus\mathcal O(n)\oplus\mathcal O\bigr).
\]

The group `H_n` acts through the base and the compatible linearizations of the two equal `O(n)` summands.  Let

\[
z(u,v,w)=(v,u,w),\qquad G_n=\langle z\rangle\times H_n.
\]

Then:

1. `S_n` is a smooth rational projective surface carrying a faithful `G_n`-action;
2. `S_n^A` is nonempty for every abelian subgroup `A <= G_n`;
3. every Sylow subgroup of `G_n` has a fixed point;
4. `S_n^{G_n}` is empty;
5. `S_n` is not weakly `G_n`-versal, hence not `G_n`-unirational.

The smallest case is

\[
S_3:\ uv=(x^6+y^6)w^2,
\qquad G_3\simeq C_2\times S_3.
\]

## 1. Smoothness and rationality

Locally on the base the equation is

\[
uv=f(t)w^2.
\]

A singular point would satisfy

\[
u=v=0,\quad f(t)w=0,\quad f'(t)w^2=0.
\]

Since `[u:v:w]` is a projective point, `w` is nonzero there; hence `f=f'=0`.  But `x^{2n}+y^{2n}` has `2n` distinct zeros in characteristic zero.  Thus `S_n` is smooth.

The conic bundle has the two disjoint sections

\[
[1:0:0],\qquad [0:1:0].
\]

A conic over the function field of `P^1` with a rational point is rational, so `S_n` is birational to a ruled surface and is rational.

Faithfulness is immediate: `H_n` acts faithfully on the base and `z` is nontrivial on a general fiber.

## 2. The central fixed curve

A projective point fixed by `z` with `w != 0` has `u=v`; substituting gives

\[
C_n=S_n^z:\quad q^2=x^{2n}+y^{2n}.
\]

The two points with `w=0` are exchanged by `z`, and the `-1` projective eigendirection would force `u=v=0`.  Therefore `S_n^z=C_n` exactly.

The map `C_n -> P^1` is a double cover branched at the `2n` simple zeros of `F_n`.  Riemann-Hurwitz gives

\[
g(C_n)=n-1\ge2.
\]

In particular `C_n` contains no rational curve.

## 3. No full fixed point

A point of `C_n` fixed by all of `H_n` would project to a point of `P^1` fixed by all of `H_n`.  The rotation fixes precisely `[1:0]` and `[0:1]`, while the reflection exchanges them.  Hence

\[
C_n^{H_n}=\varnothing.
\]

Since every `G_n`-fixed point must first be `z`-fixed,

\[
S_n^{G_n}=C_n^{H_n}=\varnothing.
\]

## 4. Condition (A)

Let `A <= G_n` be abelian and let `B` be its projection to `H_n`.  Because `n` is odd, every abelian subgroup of `D_{2n}` is cyclic: it is contained in the rotation subgroup or is generated by one reflection.

The central involution `z` acts trivially on `C_n`, so it suffices to exhibit a point of `C_n^B`.

### Rotation subgroups

Every subgroup of `<r>` fixes the two base points `[1:0]` and `[0:1]`.  The points of `C_n` above them satisfy `q^2=1`; with the displayed lift of `H_n`, these points are fixed by the rotation subgroup.

### Reflection subgroups

Write a reflection as `r^j s`.  In the affine coordinate `t=x/y`, it acts by

\[
t\longmapsto\zeta_n^j/t.
\]

Choose `alpha=\zeta_n^k` with `2k=j mod n`; this is possible because `n` is odd.  Then `alpha` is a fixed base point and `alpha^n=1`.  Since

\[
F_n(\alpha,1)=\alpha^{2n}+1=2,
\]

the two points with `q^2=2` lie over it, and the weighted projective scaling has `alpha^n=1`; at least one, in fact both for the compatible lift, is fixed.

Thus `C_n^B` is nonempty for every possible projection `B`, and every element of `A` acts on `C_n` through `B`.  Therefore

\[
S_n^A\ne\varnothing
\]

for every abelian `A <= G_n`.  Condition (A) passes.

## 5. The obstruction

Apply the central fixed-curve corollary with the central involution `z`:

* `S_n^z=C_n` is a smooth curve of genus `n-1 >= 2`;
* `S_n^{G_n}=empty`.

Following a projectivized `z`-eigenspace of any faithful linear source through an equivariant resolution gives a `G_n`-stable RCC fixed stratum.  Its image in `C_n` is a point, and `G_n`-stability would make that point `G_n`-fixed.  No such point exists.

Hence there is no `G_n`-equivariant rational map from any faithful linear source to `S_n`, even a nondominant one.  The action is not weakly `G_n`-versal and is not `G_n`-unirational.

## 6. Why the standard cohomological tests are silent

Every Sylow subgroup fixes a point on `C_n`:

* an odd Sylow subgroup lies in the rotation group and fixes a point above `[1:0]`;
* a Sylow 2-subgroup is `<z,r^j s> ~= V4` and fixes a point above a reflection-fixed base point.

Therefore the universal-torsor/ordinary Amitsur class restricts to zero on every Sylow subgroup.  Restriction-corestriction and the gcd-one set of Sylow indices force the class itself to vanish.  The higher-Amitsur theorem used in Problem F then gives vanishing in every higher degree.

For the headline case `n=3`, `G_3=C2 x S3` also has trivial Bogomolov multiplier.  Thus this theorem is not a repackaging of a known Brauer obstruction.

## 7. Literature boundary

The model is a standard de Jonquieres/conic-bundle action and lies inside the finite-group classifications of rational surfaces.  Those classifications address equivariant minimality and birational conjugacy.  Duncan's Condition-(A) sufficiency theorem covers del Pezzo surfaces of degree at least three, not the conic-bundle branch.  The exact weak-versality or `G_n`-unirationality question for this action was not found decided in the primary or citing literature through 2026-08-09.
