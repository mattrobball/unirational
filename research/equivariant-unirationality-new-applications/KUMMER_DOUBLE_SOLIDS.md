# Kummer quartic double solids

## 1. Current boundary

Let \(X\to\mathbf P^3\) be a double cover branched over a Kummer quartic surface and let \(\widetilde X\) be the standard smooth model obtained from the 16 nodes. These threefolds are rational and carry large explicit finite automorphism groups.

The 2026 higher-Amitsur analysis is decisive for specific Q8-containing actions: under Condition (A), the named Q8 subgroups give

\[
\operatorname{Am}^3(\widetilde X,Q_8)\simeq\mathbf Z/2,
\]

so those actions are already not equivariantly unirational. They should not be presented as new geometric applications.

## 2. Why the naive deck argument fails

The central deck involution fixes the resolved Kummer K3 surface. That surface contains the classical configuration of

- 16 exceptional curves over the nodes;
- 16 trope curves;
- incidence type \(16_6\).

Thus the fixed surface contains many rational curves. The original central theorem's convenient hypothesis fails.

The residual-RCC theorem gives the correct replacement: one must determine whether the residual group preserves an irreducible rational curve, not merely whether rational curves exist.

## 3. Specific non-Q8 test subgroup

For

\[
X_1=\{w^2=x_1^4+x_2^4+x_3^4+x_4^4-4ix_1x_2x_3x_4\},
\]

the known automorphism group is

\[
C_2^{\rm deck}\times(C_4^2\rtimes S_4).
\]

Take

\[
G_0=C_2^{\rm deck}\times(C_4^2\rtimes C_3),
\]

where \(C_3\) is generated by a 3-cycle in \(S_4\). Its 2-Sylow is abelian, so it contains none of the Q8 subgroups isolated by the known \(\operatorname{Am}^3\) theorem.

This is a concrete action for which the cohomological theorem is silent. It is not yet known from this audit whether Condition (A) passes.

## 4. Required finite calculation

The following finite calculation decides whether the residual-RCC theorem has a chance to apply.

1. Build the permutation action of \(C_4^2\rtimes C_3\) on the 16 nodes and 16 tropes.
2. Compute the orbit and stabilizer of every one of the 32 rational curves.
3. Determine whether any single curve is \(G_0/\langle\tau\rangle\)-stable.
4. Determine whether a stable connected union has an invariant rational component that can receive an irreducible RCC image.
5. Compute \(\widetilde X^{G_0}\).
6. Enumerate abelian subgroups and verify Condition (A).

If no residual-stable rational curve exists and \(\widetilde X^{G_0}=\varnothing\), the generalized central theorem proves non-weak-versality immediately.

If a stable curve does exist, its incidence with the remaining 31 curves becomes the input for a fixed-network argument rather than a reason to abandon the case.

## 5. Answer to the Kummer question

> Can Kummer quartic double solids be killed geometrically despite rational curves in the deck-fixed locus?

**Not uniformly.** The residual-RCC theorem shows precisely how they could be killed: prove that the residual group preserves no irreducible rational curve or other positive-dimensional RCC subvariety. For Q8-containing actions this would only reprove an existing cohomological theorem. The subgroup \(G_0\) above is the smallest high-value non-Q8 stress test, but its finite curve-permutation and Condition-(A) audits remain open.
