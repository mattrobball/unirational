Primary papers are listed by the role they play in the theorem and candidate boundaries.

## B. Versality and equivariant unirationality

1. Alexander Duncan and Zinovy Reichstein, **Versality of algebraic group actions and rational points on twisted varieties**, arXiv:1109.6093.  
   Weak, versal, and very versal actions; generic-torsor dictionary.

2. Alexander Duncan, **Equivariant unirationality of del Pezzo surfaces of degree 3 and 4**, arXiv:1410.8434; *European Journal of Mathematics* 2 (2016), 897–916.  
   Condition (A), positive results in degree at least 3, and the degree-2 counterexample involving the Geiser factor.

3. Ivan Cheltsov, Yuri Tschinkel, and Zhijia Zhang, **Equivariant unirationality of Fano threefolds**, arXiv:2502.19598, current manuscript.  
   Current positive toolbox and remaining Fano-threefold boundary.

4. Yuri Tschinkel and Zhijia Zhang, **Cohomological obstructions to equivariant unirationality**, arXiv:2504.10204; *International Mathematics Research Notices* (2026).  
   Higher-Amitsur calculations for del Pezzo surfaces and Kummer quartic double solids.

5. Federico Scavia, Yuri Tschinkel, and Zhijia Zhang, **Birational invariance of higher Amitsur groups**, arXiv:2605.02763.  
   Stable \(G\)-birational invariance of all higher Amitsur groups and the implication from vanishing of the equivariant universal-torsor obstruction.

6. Andrew Kresch and Yuri Tschinkel, **Equivariant Burnside groups: structure and operations**, arXiv:2105.02929, together with their companion Burnside papers.  
   Fixed-stratum birational invariants used to identify already-decided or partially covered cases.

## C. Smooth quartic surfaces and double solids

1. Jose Avila, Guillermo Ortiz, and Sergio Troncoso, **Invariant Smooth Quartic Surfaces by all Finite Primitive Groups of \(\operatorname{PGL}_4(\mathbf C)\)**, arXiv:2211.13273.  
   Theorem A(4) gives the unique smooth quartic invariant under the primitive \(\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbf F_7)\)-action:
   \[
   2x_0^4+6x_0x_1x_2x_3+x_1x_3^3+x_1^3x_2+x_2^3x_3=0.
   \]

2. Ivan Cheltsov, **Kummer quartic double solids**, arXiv:2202.11668.  
   Automorphisms and equivariant birational rigidity of Kummer double solids.

3. Yuri Tschinkel and Zhijia Zhang, arXiv:2504.10204, above.  
   The third-Amitsur obstruction for selected Q8 actions on the special Kummer model.

4. Classical quartic-double-solid unirationality references, as summarized in the modern Fano-threefold literature.  
   Only ordinary unirationality is used; no rationality claim is made for the smooth double solid in this packet.

## D. Conic-bundle surfaces and the plane Cremona group

1. Igor Dolgachev and Vasily Iskovskikh, **Finite subgroups of the plane Cremona group**, arXiv:math/0610595.  
   Exceptional conic-bundle model, automorphism structure, and \(G\)-minimal surface classification.

2. Andrey Pinardin, **\(G\)-solid rational surfaces**, arXiv:2309.13757; *European Journal of Mathematics* 10 (2024).  
   Modern equivariant Sarkisov/solidity boundary for rational conic bundles.

3. Andrey Pinardin, Arman Sarikyan, and Egor Yasinsky, **Linearization problem for finite subgroups of the plane Cremona group**, current published/preprint version.  
   Linearizability classification; used only to avoid confusing nonlinearizability with non-\(G\)-unirationality.

## E. Rational Fano conic-bundle threefolds

1. Shuto Abe, **Automorphism groups and linearizability of rational Fano conic bundle threefolds**, arXiv:2506.15042, current 2026 revision.  
   Rationality of Mori–Mukai No. 2.18; double-cover, conic-bundle, and quadric-bundle models; automorphism formulas; special high-symmetry examples; equivariant intermediate-Jacobian torsor obstruction to linearizability.

2. Tudor Ciurca, Sho Tanimoto, and Yuri Tschinkel, **Intermediate Jacobians and linearizability**, cited and generalized by Abe.  
   Equivariant IJ-torsor obstruction; it controls linearizability rather than equivariant unirationality.

3. Ivan Cheltsov, Yuri Tschinkel, and Zhijia Zhang, arXiv:2502.19598, above.  
   General equivariant-unirationality context for Fano threefolds.

## F. Index-one Fano threefolds

1. Alexander Kuznetsov and Yuri Prokhorov, **Prime Fano threefolds of genus 12 with a \(\mathbf G_m\)-action**, arXiv:1711.08504.  
   Explicit genus-12 models, isomorphism classes, and automorphism groups.

2. Mikio Furushima, **Mukai–Umemura's example of the Fano threefold with genus 12 as a compactification of \(\mathbf C^3\)**, *Nagoya Mathematical Journal* 127 (1992), 145–165.  
   Rationality of the Mukai–Umemura threefold.

3. Tetsushi Ito, Akihiro Kanemitsu, Teppei Takamatsu, and Yuuji Tanaka, **Fano threefolds of genus 12 with large automorphism group in positive and mixed characteristic**, arXiv:2601.10106.  
   Current classification context for Mukai–Umemura type.

4. Kiryong Chung and Jeong-Seop Kim, **Rational quartic curves in the Mukai–Umemura variety**, 2025 journal version, and **A remark on rational quartic curves in prime Fano threefolds of degree 22**, arXiv:2606.18102.  
   Current explicit curve geometry; no involution/\(V_4\) fixed-scheme table suitable for the centralizer theorem was found.

5. The repository's `v14_not_weakly_versal.tex`.  
   First sealed index-one centralizer application.

## G. Del Pezzo and singular-Fano classification boundaries

1. Ivan Cheltsov, Yuri Tschinkel, and Zhijia Zhang, **Birational geometry of actions on del Pezzo surfaces**, arXiv:2604.20425.  
   Current birational classification of regular generically free finite-group actions on del Pezzo surfaces.

2. Ivan Cheltsov, Yuri Tschinkel, and Zhijia Zhang, **Equivariant geometry of the Segre cubic and the Burkhardt quartic**, arXiv:2308.15271, current revision.  
   Rational singular-Fano actions and linearizability/Burnside boundaries.

3. Yuri Prokhorov, Constantin Shramov, Trepalin, Blanc, Yasinsky, Avilov, and collaborators, classification papers cited in the two works above.  
   Used to locate explicit Mori fiber actions; no statement from this cluster was treated as an equivariant-unirationality theorem unless it explicitly addressed versality or domination by a linear source.
