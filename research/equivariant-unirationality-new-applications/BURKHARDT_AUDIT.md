# Burkhardt quartic obstruction audit

## 1. Literature boundary

Cheltsov--Tschinkel--Zhang settle linearizability for all but four
conjugacy classes of subgroups of
`PSp_4(F_3)=Aut(X_B)`. The unresolved abstract groups are
`S_3`, `D_5`, `D_6`, and `C_3 rtimes C_4` (with the exact embeddings given
in their Section 7). Their question is linearizability, not
`G`-unirationality. This packet therefore labels these actions
**PARTIALLY-COVERED**, not OPEN-CONFIRMED for the present question.

Use the standard equation

\[
X_B:\quad y_1(y_1^3+y_2^3+y_3^3+y_4^3+y_5^3)
       +3y_2y_3y_4y_5=0.
\]

## 2. The `C_3 rtimes C_4` center

Let `G=<sigma_3,sigma_4>` be the published embedding and
`z=sigma_4^2`. The element `z` is central.

Fix a primitive cube root `q`, so `q^2+q+1=0`. On the positive
`z`-eigenspace one may write

\[
y_2=qy_3,\qquad y_4=qy_5.
\]

and the section of `X_B` is

\[
D:\quad a^4+2ab^3+2ac^3+3q^2b^2c^2=0. \tag{1}
\]

The singular equations are

\[
4a^3+2b^3+2c^3=0,\quad
6b(ab+q^2c^2)=0,\quad
6c(ac+q^2b^2)=0.
\]

Neither `b` nor `c` can vanish at a projective singular point. After setting
`b=1`, they force `c=t`, `t^3=1`, and `a=-q^2t^2`. Hence the singular locus
is exactly

\[
[-q^2t^2:1:t],\qquad t^3=1.
\]

In the affine chart `b=1`, the determinant of the Hessian in `(a,c)` at
such a point is `-108t`, so all three singularities are ordinary nodes.
Since a plane quartic has arithmetic genus three, the normalization of `D`
has genus zero. The negative eigenspace contributes two points. The exact
calculation is replayed by `verify_burkhardt_nearmiss.py`.

Thus `X_B^z` contains a rational curve. More importantly, this component
is preserved by the residual action coming from the central element. It is
therefore an admissible RCC image of the selected source stratum. Neither
the old central theorem nor Theorem G1 applies.

## 3. Coordinate-transposition lane

In the six-coordinate model

\[
e_1(x_1,\ldots,x_6)=e_4(x_1,\ldots,x_6)=0,
\]

a coordinate transposition has anti-invariant line generated by

\[
p=[1:-1:0:0:0:0].
\]

Direct substitution gives `e_1(p)=e_4(p)=0`, so `p in X_B`. The
anti-invariant eigenspace is one-dimensional. Every projective
transformation centralizing the involution preserves this line (the two
eigenspaces have unequal dimensions and therefore cannot be interchanged),
so the entire centralizer fixes `p`. Hence the deeper fixed locus is
nonempty, and the centralizer theorem fails in this lane as well.

## 4. Consequences

The audit rules out two tempting shortcuts:

- the central involution of the open `C_3 rtimes C_4` action does **not**
  have positive-genus fixed curve;
- a coordinate transposition does **not** have empty centralizer fixed
  locus.

No negative `G`-unirationality theorem is claimed. A viable Burkhardt proof
would need one of:

1. a different element with a non-RCC residual fixed component;
2. an incidence theorem showing that the rational fixed quartic cannot be
   the image because of adjacent fixed strata;
3. a torsor or quotient argument not visible in the current fixed-locus
   test.

This is a useful near-miss because the exact failure is geometric rather
than merely computational.
