# The Fermat degree-two del Pezzo and its index-one Fano product

## Statement

Let

\[
S_F=\{w^2=x^4+y^4+z^4\}\subset\mathbf P(2,1,1,1),
\]

let `tau(w,x,y,z)=(-w,x,y,z)` be the Geiser involution, and let `S_3`
permute `x,y,z`. Put

\[
G_F=\langle\tau\rangle\times S_3.
\]

Then:

\[
\boxed{S_F\text{ satisfies Condition (A), but is not weakly }
G_F\text{-versal}.}
\]

In particular `S_F` is not `G_F`-unirational. The universal-torsor
obstruction and all higher Amitsur groups vanish for the action and every
subgroup.

The product

\[
Y_F=\mathbf P^1\times S_F
\]

with trivial action on `P^1` is a smooth rational Fano threefold of index
one and is likewise not weakly `G_F`-versal.

## 1. Central fixed curve and deeper fixed locus

The fixed locus of `tau` is the branch curve

\[
B_F=\{x^4+y^4+z^4=0\}\subset\mathbf P^2,
\]

a smooth plane quartic of genus three.

A projective point fixed by all coordinate permutations must be
`[1:1:1]`. It is not on `B_F`, since its defining polynomial has value
`3`. Every `G_F`-fixed point would have to be `tau`-fixed and hence lie on
`B_F`. Therefore

\[
S_F^{G_F}=B_F^{S_3}=\varnothing. \tag{1}
\]

Corollary T3.1 now implies the negative theorem for `S_F` once Condition
(A) is checked.

## 2. Condition (A)

Let `A<=G_F` be abelian. Enlarge it by the central involution `tau`; a
fixed point of the enlarged group is a fixed point of `A`. Thus it is
enough to consider abelian subgroups of `S_3`, which are trivial, `C_2`,
or `C_3`.

- A transposition, say `(xy)`, fixes the line `x=y`. Its intersection with
  `B_F` is

  \[
  2x^4+z^4=0,
  \]

  which has four points over the algebraically closed ground field.
- A 3-cycle has eigenpoints

  \[
  [1:\omega:\omega^2],\qquad[1:\omega^2:\omega].
  \]

  Since fourth powers preserve the exponents modulo three,
  `1+omega+omega^2=0` shows that both lie on `B_F`.

Hence every abelian subgroup of `G_F` has a fixed point on `S_F`, and
Condition (A) holds.

## 3. Cohomological silence

A 2-Sylow of `G_F` is `C_2(tau) x C_2` generated by `tau` and a
transposition; it fixes any point of the corresponding line-section of
`B_F`. A 3-Sylow fixes the two 3-cycle eigenpoints above. Thus every Sylow
subgroup fixes a point.

The restriction-corestriction argument kills the equivariant
universal-torsor obstruction for `G_F` and every subgroup. The Picard group
of a del Pezzo surface is free, so Scavia--Tschinkel--Zhang then kills all
higher Amitsur groups in degrees at least two. Moreover `C_3` is cyclic and normal in `G_F`, with abelian quotient
`C_2 x C_2`; the same holds after passage to subgroups. Bogomolov's
cyclic-by-abelian lemma therefore gives `B_0(H)=0` for every
`H<=G_F`. In particular this action is not one of the quaternionic
`Am^3` cases in the current classification.

## 4. The index-one Fano product

The product `Y_F=P^1 x S_F` is smooth and rational, and

\[
-K_{Y_F}=2H+(-K_{S_F})
\]

is ample. The class `-K_{S_F}` is primitive in `Pic(S_F)` (in the blowup
model its coefficients are `3,-1,...,-1`), so the Fano index of `Y_F` is
one.

Its central fixed locus is

\[
Y_F^\tau=\mathbf P^1\times B_F.
\]

This fixed divisor contains many rational curves, so the original
no-rational-curve hypothesis fails. However, every irreducible RCC
subvariety of `P^1 x B_F` maps to a point of the genus-three curve `B_F`.
If it were `G_F`-stable, that point would be fixed by `S_3`, contradicting
(1). The residual-RCC theorem therefore proves that `Y_F` is not weakly
`G_F`-versal.

This gives an index-one Fano application of the refined theorem. It is not
presented as a prime Picard-rank-one Fano analogue of `V_14`; its value is
that the central fixed divisor contains rational curves and nevertheless
cannot receive the selected residual-stable RCC source stratum.
