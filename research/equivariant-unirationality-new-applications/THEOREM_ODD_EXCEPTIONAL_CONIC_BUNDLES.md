# An infinite family of rational conic bundles that satisfy Condition (A) but are not weakly versal

## 1. Exceptional conic bundles

Fix an odd integer \(g\ge3\). In the weighted projective space

\[
\mathbf P(1,1,g+1,g+1)_{T_0,T_1,T_2,T_3}
\]

consider

\[
Y_g=\left\{
T_0T_1(T_0^{2g}+T_1^{2g})+T_2T_3=0
\right\}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

The surface \(Y_g\) has the two standard cyclic quotient points at
\([0:0:1:0]\) and \([0:0:0:1]\). Let

\[
S_g\longrightarrow Y_g
\]

be its minimal resolution. This is the exceptional conic bundle described in the Dolgachev–Iskovskikh classification. Projection to \([T_0:T_1]\) gives a conic bundle over \(\mathbf P^1\) with \(2g+2\) singular fibers and two sections. In particular, \(S_g\) is rational.

## 2. The group action

Let \(\xi\) be a primitive \(2g\)-th root of unity. Define

\[
r(T_0,T_1,T_2,T_3)
=(\xi T_0,\xi^{-1}T_1,T_2,T_3),
\tag{2.1}
\]

\[
s(T_0,T_1,T_2,T_3)
=(T_1,T_0,T_2,T_3),
\tag{2.2}
\]

and

\[
j(T_0,T_1,T_2,T_3)
=(T_0,T_1,T_3,T_2).
\tag{2.3}
\]

The defining equation is invariant. Since \(g+1\) is even, \(r^g\) is weighted-projectively the scalar \(-1\); hence \(r\) has order \(g\) on \(Y_g\). Moreover,

\[
srs=r^{-1}.
\]

Thus \(r,s\) generate the dihedral group \(D_{2g}\) of order \(2g\), and \(j\) is a central involution commuting with it. All automorphisms lift uniquely to the minimal resolution. Put

\[
G_g=D_{2g}\times\langle j\rangle.
\tag{2.4}
\]

## 3. Main theorem

> **Theorem.** For every odd \(g\ge3\), the rational surface \(S_g\) with the action (2.4) satisfies Condition (A), but
> \[
> \boxed{S_g\text{ is not weakly }G_g\text{-versal}.}
> \]
> Consequently \(S_g\) is not \(G_g\)-unirational.

This answers the conic-bundle test posed by the application program: Condition (A) is not sufficient for equivariant unirationality even on rational \(G\)-Mori conic-bundle surfaces.

## 4. Fixed locus of the central involution

Away from the two quotient points, a point fixed by \(j\) has

\[
T_2=T_3=U.
\]

Its fixed curve is therefore

\[
C_g=\left\{
U^2=-T_0T_1(T_0^{2g}+T_1^{2g})
\right\}
\subset\mathbf P(1,1,g+1).
\tag{4.1}
\]

The two quotient points are exchanged by \(j\), so the exceptional curves over them are exchanged and contribute no fixed component on \(S_g\). Hence

\[
S_g^j=C_g.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The binary form

\[
T_0T_1(T_0^{2g}+T_1^{2g})
\]

has \(2g+2\) distinct roots on \(\mathbf P^1\). Thus \(C_g\to\mathbf P^1\) is a smooth double cover branched at \(2g+2\) points, and Riemann–Hurwitz gives

\[
g(C_g)=g.
\tag{4.3}
\]

In particular, \(S_g^j\) contains no rational curve.

## 5. No global fixed point

Any point of \(S_g^{G_g}\) lies in \(C_g\), because it is fixed by \(j\). Its image in the base must be fixed by the full dihedral group \(D_{2g}\).

The rotation subgroup fixes exactly \(0\) and \(\infty\) on \(\mathbf P^1\), while the reflection \(s\) exchanges them. Therefore

\[
(\mathbf P^1)^{D_{2g}}=\varnothing,
\]

and hence

\[
S_g^{G_g}=\varnothing.
\tag{5.1}
\]

## 6. Condition (A)

Since \(g\) is odd, every abelian subgroup of \(D_{2g}\) is either contained in the rotation subgroup or generated by one reflection. Let \(A\le G_g\) be abelian.

### Rotation type

If the projection of \(A\) to \(D_{2g}\) is contained in \(\langle r\rangle\), use either branch point over \(T_0=0\) or \(T_1=0\). These points lie on \(C_g\), are fixed by every rotation, and are fixed by \(j\).

### Reflection type

If the projection is generated by a reflection \(h=sr^k\), then \(h\) has two eigendirections on the base, with eigenvalues \(\pm1\). Above either eigendirection, the two points of \(C_g\) are fixed by \(h\): the fiber coordinate has weight \(g+1\), and

\[
(-1)^{g+1}=1.
\]

They are also fixed by \(j\). Thus every subgroup of \(\langle h,j\rangle\simeq C_2^2\) has a fixed point.

These cases exhaust the abelian subgroups, proving

\[
S_g^A\neq\varnothing
\quad\text{for all abelian }A\le G_g.
\tag{6.1}
\]

## 7. Central obstruction

The involution \(j\) is central, its fixed locus is the genus-\(g\) curve \(C_g\), and \(S_g^{G_g}=\varnothing\). The original central fixed-locus theorem from `problems/E-klein-cubic/theory/FIX_T_gate.md` applies directly: a surviving projectivized \(j\)-eigenspace from any faithful linear source is rationally chain connected, so its image in \(C_g\) is a point; equivariance would make that point \(G_g\)-fixed, contradicting (5.1).

Therefore no faithful linear source maps equivariantly to \(S_g\), and the action is not weakly versal.

## 8. Literature boundary and novelty

Dolgachev–Iskovskikh give the weighted model, the exceptional-conic-bundle structure, and its automorphisms. Later equivariant Sarkisov and \(G\)-solid-surface work classifies the birational position of such actions, but does not ask weak versality or equivariant unirationality.

Nonlinearizability and \(G\)-solidity do not imply the theorem above. The new conclusion is stronger and is obtained from the positive-genus fixed curve. No previous source found states this family of non-weakly-versal actions.

The standard cohomological obstructions have not been uniformly computed for the entire family; this packet makes no claim that all higher Amitsur groups vanish. The important comparison is that Condition (A) passes exactly.
