# A smooth quartic double solid with vanishing cohomological obstructions that is not weakly versal

## 1. The action

Let \(\zeta\) be a primitive seventh root of unity and set

\[
B(x)=2x_0^4+6x_0x_1x_2x_3+x_1x_3^3+x_1^3x_2+x_2^3x_3.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Avila–Ortiz–Troncoso prove that \(B=0\) is the unique smooth quartic surface invariant under the primitive \(\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbf F_7)\)-action on \(\mathbf P^3\).

Consider the smooth quartic double solid

\[
X=\{w^2=B(x)\}\subset\mathbf P(1,1,1,1,2).
\tag{1.2}
\]

Let \(H=C_7\rtimes C_3\) be generated by

\[
a(x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3)
=(x_0,\zeta^4x_1,\zeta^2x_2,\zeta x_3),
\tag{1.3}
\]

and

\[
b(x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3)=(x_0,x_2,x_3,x_1).
\tag{1.4}
\]

Then \(bab^{-1}=a^4\). Both transformations preserve (1.1), and they act on \(X\) with \(w\) unchanged. Let

\[
\tau(x,w)=(x,-w)
\]

be the deck involution and put

\[
G=H\times\langle\tau\rangle.
\tag{1.5}
\]

The action is faithful and generically free.

## 2. Main theorem

> **Theorem.** The action (1.5) satisfies Condition (A) and has vanishing equivariant universal-torsor obstruction. Consequently all its higher Amitsur groups vanish. Nevertheless
> \[
> \boxed{X\text{ is not weakly }G\text{-versal}.}
> \]
> In particular, \(X\) is not \(G\)-unirational.

The underlying threefold is a smooth Fano threefold of index \(2\) and degree \(2\). It is classically unirational. Thus the theorem is a genuinely equivariant failure of unirationality.

## 3. Exact fixed loci

The deck involution is central and

\[
X^\tau=B.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The four \(a\)-eigenlines in \(\mathbf P^3\) are the coordinate points. Evaluating (1.1) gives

\[
B(e_0)=2,\qquad B(e_1)=B(e_2)=B(e_3)=0.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Hence

\[
B^{C_7}=\{e_1,e_2,e_3\}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

The transformation \(b\) cycles \(e_1,e_2,e_3\), while fixing \(e_0\). Since \(e_0\notin B\),

\[
B^H=\varnothing.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Therefore

\[
X^G=(X^\tau)^H=B^H=\varnothing.
\tag{3.5}
\]

The \(1\)-eigenspace of \(b\) is the projective line

\[
[x_0:x_1:x_2:x_3]=[s:t:t:t].
\]

On this line,

\[
B=2s^4+6st^3+3t^4,
\tag{3.6}
\]

so \(B^{C_3}\neq\varnothing\).

## 4. Condition (A)

Every abelian subgroup of the nonabelian group \(H=C_7\rtimes C_3\) is contained in the normal \(C_7\) or in a conjugate of \(C_3\). Equations (3.3) and (3.6) give a branch point fixed by each such subgroup.

Let \(A\le G=H\times C_2\) be abelian. Its projection to \(H\) is abelian. Choose a point of \(B\) fixed by that projection. Since the deck involution acts trivially on \(B\), the same point is fixed by all of \(A\). Thus

\[
X^A\neq\varnothing
\quad\text{for every abelian }A\le G.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Condition (A) passes.

## 5. No residual-stable rational curve on the fixed K3 surface

The surface \(B\) is a smooth quartic K3 surface, hence is not uniruled and is not rationally chain connected.

Suppose that \(C\subset B\) is an irreducible \(H\)-stable rational curve. The induced action on its normalization gives

\[
H/K\hookrightarrow\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbf C)
\tag{5.1}
\]

for a normal subgroup \(K\triangleleft H\). The normal subgroups of \(H\) are

\[
1,\qquad C_7,\qquad H.
\]

- If \(K=1\), then \(H\) embeds into \(\operatorname{PGL}_2\), impossible because the nonabelian group of order \(21\) is neither cyclic, dihedral, \(A_4\), \(S_4\), nor \(A_5\).
- If \(K=C_7\), then \(C\subset B^{C_7}\), contradicting (3.3).
- If \(K=H\), then \(C\subset B^H\), contradicting (3.4).

Therefore

\[
B\text{ contains no }H\text{-stable rational curve}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Any positive-dimensional irreducible RCC subvariety of the K3 surface \(B\) would be either a rational curve or all of \(B\). The first is excluded by (5.2), and the second is not RCC. Hence every \(G\)-stable irreducible RCC subvariety of \(X^\tau=B\) is a point.

## 6. Fixed-locus obstruction

Apply the residual-RCC central obstruction in `GENERALIZATIONS.md` with the central element \(\tau\). Its two hypotheses are:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{every }G\text{-stable RCC subvariety of }X^\tau\text{ is a point},\\
&X^G=\varnothing.
\end{aligned}
\]

They were proved in Sections 5 and 3. Therefore no faithful linear \(G\)-representation admits a \(G\)-equivariant rational map to \(X\). Since \(X\) is complete, it is not weakly \(G\)-versal.

## 7. The standard cohomological obstructions are silent

The Picard group of a smooth quartic double solid is

\[
\operatorname{Pic}(X)=\mathbf Z\cdot H,
\qquad H=\pi^*\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^3}(1).
\tag{7.1}
\]

The explicit linear action (1.3)–(1.5) gives \(H\) a \(G\)-linearization. Hence the equivariant universal torsor associated to the generator exists, so the universal-torsor obstruction \(\beta(X,G)\) vanishes.

Scavia–Tschinkel–Zhang prove that, for a smooth projective \(G\)-variety with free finitely generated Picard group, vanishing of \(\beta\) implies

\[
\operatorname{Am}^n(X,G)=0
\qquad\text{for every }n\ge2.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Thus neither the ordinary Amitsur group nor any higher Amitsur group detects this action. Condition (A) also passes. The obstruction is genuinely the residual fixed geometry of the K3 branch surface.

## 8. Literature boundary

The invariant quartic and its primitive projective action are classified by Avila–Ortiz–Troncoso. The current equivariant-unirationality literature treats Fano threefolds of index at least two broadly, but does not decide this specific smooth quartic-double-solid action. The 2026 cohomological work treats Kummer quartic double solids, whose branch surfaces are singular and carry a distinguished node/trope configuration; it does not cover the smooth K3 branch in (1.1).

No source found before this packet proves or refutes weak versality for (1.5).
