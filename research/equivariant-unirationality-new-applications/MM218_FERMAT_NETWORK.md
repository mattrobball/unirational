## 2. Theorem A — the full order-192 action is decided by the abelian gate

> **Theorem A.** Let `G = Aut(X)`, `|G| = 192`. There is a subgroup
> `A \cong (Z/2)^3` of `G`, containing `tau`, with
> \[
> X^A=\varnothing .
> \]
> Hence Condition (A) fails for `G`, `X` is not weakly `G`-versal, and `X` is
> not `G`-unirational.

**The witnesses.** There are exactly six such `A`, forming a single conjugacy
class; they are precisely the *minimal* abelian subgroups with empty fixed
locus, and every abelian subgroup of `G` with empty fixed locus contains one of
them. One of them is generated by

\[
(-I_2,\ \operatorname{diag}(-1,-1,1),\ -1),\qquad
(-I_2,\ \operatorname{diag}(-1,-1,1),\ +1),\qquad
\Big(\begin{bmatrix}0&\zeta_8^{3}\\ \zeta_8&0\end{bmatrix},\
-P_{(xy)},\ -i\Big).
\]

Its fixed locus downstairs is `{[1:\pm\zeta_8^{7}]} x {[0:0:1],[1:1:0],[1:-1:0]}`,
six points, and `F` is nonzero at all six:

| `p in P^1` | `q in P^2` | `F(p,q)` |
|---|---|---|
| `[1:\zeta_8^7]` | `[0:0:1]` | `2\zeta_8^7` |
| `[1:\zeta_8^7]` | `[1:1:0]` | `2+2i` |
| `[1:\zeta_8^7]` | `[1:-1:0]` | `2+2i` |
| `[1:-\zeta_8^7]` | `[0:0:1]` | `-2\zeta_8^7` |
| `[1:-\zeta_8^7]` | `[1:1:0]` | `2+2i` |
| `[1:-\zeta_8^7]` | `[1:-1:0]` | `2+2i` |

Since `tau in A`, the character `chi = mu/(alpha beta)` has `chi(tau) = -1`, so
no point off `Z` is `A`-fixed either; `X^A = \varnothing`.

**Proof of the theorem.** Suppose `f : P(V) \dashrightarrow X` is
`G`-equivariant for a faithful linear representation `V`. Resolve
indeterminacy by a sequence of smooth `G`-equivariant blowups
`pi:\widetilde P\to P(V)` and let `\widetilde f:\widetilde P\to X`. For the
abelian `A` above, `P(V)^A \ne \varnothing` (a simultaneous eigenvector
exists). Each blowup centre is `G`-stable, hence `A`-stable; over an
`A`-fixed point of the centre the fibre is `P(N_p)` with a linear `A`-action,
which has an eigenvector, and over an `A`-fixed point off the centre the lift
is unique. Inductively `\widetilde P^A \ne \varnothing`. Then
`\widetilde f(\widetilde P^A)\subseteq X^A=\varnothing`, a contradiction. The
affine-source and generic-torsor steps are verbatim as in
`GENERALIZATIONS.md` §2. `\square`

This is the standard abelian (Reichstein–Youssin) mechanism; no new theory is
used. It answers TOP5 item 5 gate (a) **negatively for the full group**: one
cannot freeze the whole order-192 action as a Condition-(A) laboratory.

## 5. Curve census on the del Pezzo `Z`

### 5.1 The 28 bitangents of the Fermat quartic

Derivation (elimination, complete): a line with zero `z`-coefficient is
`\alpha x+\beta y = 0`; restricting gives `(\alpha^4+\beta^4)s^4+u^4`, a square
iff `\alpha^4 = -\beta^4`, so 4 lines. A line `z = ax+by` with `ab = 0` gives
`x^4+(1+b^4)y^4` (or the symmetric expression), a square iff `b^4 = -1`, so
`4+4` more. A line `z = ax+by` with `a,b \ne 0` gives, writing
`x^4+y^4+(ax+by)^4 = (px^2+qxy+ry^2)^2`,

\[
p^2 = 1+a^4,\quad q = \tfrac{2a^3b}{p},\quad r = \tfrac{b^2p}{a^2},\quad
r^2 = 1+b^4 \Rightarrow b^4 = a^4,
\]
\[
q^2+2pr = 6a^2b^2 \Rightarrow 2a^8+(1+a^4)^2 = 3a^4(1+a^4) \Rightarrow a^4 = 1,
\]

so `a^4 = b^4 = 1`, giving 16 lines. Total `4+4+4+16 = 28`, matching the
classical count.

| family | count | contact with `Delta` |
|---|---:|---|
| `\alpha x+\beta y = 0`, `(\alpha/\beta)^4 = -1` (and the two coordinate-pair analogues) | 12 | `4` (hyperflex) |
| `ax+by-z = 0`, `a^4 = b^4 = 1` | 16 | `2+2` |

Machine-verified: 28 distinct lines, multiplicity patterns `12 x (4)` and
`16 x (2+2)`.

### 5.2 The 56 `(-1)`-curves

Over `r in P^2` the two sheets of `pi_2` are the roots of
`Q_1 u^2 + 2Q_2 u + Q_3`, i.e. `u = (-Q_2 \pm \sqrt{\Delta})/Q_1`. On a
bitangent `L` we have `\Delta|_L = g_L^2` with `g_L` a binary quadratic, so
`pi_2^{-1}(L)` splits into

\[
C_L^{\pm} = \{(\,[-Q_2|_L \pm g_L : Q_1|_L],\ r\,) : r\in L\}.
\]

All 56 are computed explicitly over `K`, together with the full `56 x 56`
intersection matrix. Verified: `C^2 = -1`, `C_L^+\cdot C_L^- = 2`,
`-K_Z = C_L^+ + C_L^-` independently of `L`, `-K_Z\cdot C = 1`, `K_Z^2 = 2`,
and each curve meets exactly 27 of the other 54.

### 5.3 The conic bundle `pi_1 : Z \to P^1`

`Z \to P^1` is a conic bundle: the fibre over `[t_0:t_1]` is the conic
`t_0^2Q_1+2t_0t_1Q_2+t_1^2Q_3` in `P^2`. Its discriminant is

\[
\det\operatorname{diag}\big(i(t_0^2+t_1^2),\ t_0^2-t_1^2,\ 2t_0t_1\big)
= 2i\,t_0t_1(t_0^4-t_1^4),
\]

six distinct roots, so exactly six reducible fibres and twelve fibre
components — and those twelve components are exactly the twelve hyperflex
bitangent lifts.

### 5.4 Orbits and invariant Picard classes

| group | orbits on the 56 curves | `rk Pic(Z)^{\bullet}` |
|---|---|---:|
| `Gbar` (order 96) | `12 + 12 + 32` | 2 |
| `\bar H` (order 48) | `12 + 12 + 16 + 16` | 2 |

The two 12-orbits are the twelve fibre components (`C\cdot f = 0`) and the

with `f` the conic-bundle fibre class. **No `(-1)`-curve is stable** under

### 5.5 No `\bar H`-stable irreducible rational curve

`|-K_Z| = pi_2^*|O_{P^2}(1)|`, so a stable member of `|-K_Z|` is

`\bar H`-invariant line in `P^2` (no fixed point of the dual action), so
`|-K_Z|` has no stable member.

For all classes at once we use criterion 4.2 of `GENERALIZATIONS.md`: an
`N`-stable irreducible rational curve `C` gives `N/K \hookrightarrow PGL_2` for

of `\bar H` are exactly four:

|---:|---:|---|---:|---|

> **Proposition B1.** There is no `\bar H`-stable irreducible rational curve on

> **the only `H`-stable irreducible RCC closed subvariety of `Z` is `Z` itself.**

## 6. Theorem B — the carrier reduction, and the dichotomy

Recall the accepted survivor construction (`FIX_IX_v14.md`,
`GENERALIZATIONS.md` §2): for `sigma = tau` central and an `H`-equivariant
`f:P(V)\dashrightarrow X`, functorial equivariant resolution
`\widetilde P \to P(V)` produces an irreducible, `H`-stable, rationally chain
connected, pointwise-`tau`-fixed subvariety `F \subseteq \widetilde P`, obtained
from `F_0 = P(V_\lambda)` (`\lambda` an eigenvalue of `tau` on `V`) by a chain

\[
F = F_N \twoheadrightarrow F_{N-1}\twoheadrightarrow\cdots
\twoheadrightarrow F_0 = P(V_\lambda),
\]

each step either an equivariant birational contraction (strict transform) or the
projectivization `P(E_j)\to F_{j-1}` of a nonzero `H`-linearized bundle. Call
such an `F` a **carrier tower** over `(H,V,\lambda)`. Note `tau` acts trivially
on every `F_j`, so all of them carry `\bar H`-actions.

> **Theorem B.** Let `H \le Aut(X)` be the frozen order-96 subgroup and let
> `f : P(V)\dashrightarrow X` be `H`-equivariant for a faithful linear
> representation `V`. Then, with `\widetilde f:\widetilde P\to X` the resolved
> morphism and `F` the `tau`-carrier:
>
> 1. `\widetilde f(F) = Z = X^\tau`: the carrier **surjects onto the whole
>    deck-fixed degree-two del Pezzo**;
> 2. `\widetilde f(\widetilde P) \in \{Z,\ X\}`: the resolved image is either
>    exactly `Z` or all of `X`.

**Proof.** `\widetilde f(F)` is an irreducible, `H`-stable, rationally chain
connected closed subvariety of `X^\tau = Z`. By Proposition B1 it equals `Z`.
This is (1). For (2), `\widetilde f(\widetilde P)` is an irreducible closed
subvariety of `X` containing `Z`, and `Z` is an irreducible divisor on the
irreducible threefold `X`, so the only such subvarieties are `Z` and `X`.
`\square`

Theorem B is the exact three-dimensional analogue of the Klein-side reduction:
it converts "no carrier" into "the carrier is everything", pinning the remaining
question to a dominance statement rather than a fixed-locus statement.

### 6.1 Proposition C — the surface branch is obstructed for `\bar H`-sources

> **Proposition C.** Condition (A) fails for the `\bar H`-action on the surface
> `Z`. Explicitly there is an abelian `\bar A \le \bar H` of order 16 with
> `Z^{\bar A} = \varnothing`; its preimage `A' \le H` has order 32, is
> nonabelian, and `[A',A'] = \langle\tau\rangle`. Consequently `Z` is not
> weakly `\bar H`-versal: there is no `\bar H`-equivariant rational map
> `P(W)\dashrightarrow Z` from a faithful linear `\bar H`-representation `W`.

(Proof: as for Theorem A, applied on the smooth projective surface `Z` with the
faithful group `\bar H`.)

Proposition C is what makes the remaining gap sharp rather than vague. Branch
(2) of Theorem B — `\widetilde f(\widetilde P) = Z` — would give an
`H`-equivariant rational map `P(V)\dashrightarrow Z` with `tau` acting trivially
on the target. If the source were a *linear `\bar H`-representation*, Proposition
C would close it immediately. It is not: `V` is a linear `H`-representation, and
the relevant test subgroup `A'` is nonabelian with `tau in [A',A']`. This is
precisely the crossing that the network lemma must make.

### 6.2 Why the naive transfer fails, exactly

For `A' \le H` with `[A',A'] = \langle\tau\rangle`:

* every character `chi` of `A'` satisfies `chi(tau) = 1`, so `P(V)^{A'}\ne
  \varnothing` iff `V_{+}|_{A'}` contains a character. In particular, if `V_+ =
  0` (i.e. `tau` acts as the scalar `-1` on all of `V`) then `P(V)^{A'} =
  \varnothing` and nothing is forced;
* even when `P(V)^{A'}\ne\varnothing`, `A'` is nonabelian, so the fibre
  `P(N_p)` of a blowup need not contain an `A'`-fixed point: `A'`-fixed points
  are not preserved by equivariant blowups, and the Reichstein–Youssin
  induction breaks at the first step.

So the missing input is exactly a *survival* statement for a distinguished
nonabelian subgroup along a carrier tower.

## 8. The boxed gap

> **Missing implication (marker `MM218-NETWORK-GAP`).**
>
> \[
> \boxed{
> \begin{aligned}
> &\text{Let } H \text{ be a finite group, } \tau\in Z(H) \text{ of order }2,\
> V \text{ a faithful linear } H\text{-representation, }\lambda \text{ an
> eigenvalue of }\tau\text{ on }V.\\
> &\text{Let } F \text{ be a carrier tower over } (H,V,\lambda)
> \text{ (§6), and let } S \text{ be a smooth projective } H\text{-surface with}\\
> &\qquad S^{H}=\varnothing
> \quad\text{and}\quad
> \text{the only }H\text{-stable irreducible RCC closed subvariety of }S
> \text{ is } S.\\
> &\text{Assume moreover that } \bar H = H/\langle\tau\rangle \text{ acts
> faithfully on } S \text{ and that Condition (A) fails for } \bar H
> \text{ on } S.\\[2pt]
> &\textbf{Then there is no } H\text{-equivariant surjection } F \twoheadrightarrow S .
> \end{aligned}}
> \]

Granting the boxed statement, Theorem B(1) is contradicted and the order-96
action on the Fermat No. 2.18 threefold is not weakly `H`-versal, hence not
`H`-unirational; the same applies verbatim to Abe's order-12 example.

**What a proof must supply.** By §6.2 the only route through the present data is
a survival statement: for the distinguished nonabelian `A' \le H` with
`[A',A'] = \langle\tau\rangle` and `S^{\bar A'} = \varnothing`, one must show
that a carrier tower cannot avoid `A'`-fixed points — equivalently that the
`H`-linearized bundles `E_j` and the blowup normal spaces along the tower cannot
all be `A'`-representations without a character. That is a statement about
linearized bundles on the tower, not about the geometry of `S`; the incidence
table of §7 is the datum that any such argument must contradict.

**Boundary.** Nothing here invokes the withdrawn claim that every fixed stratum
on every model remains RCC, and no Chow projectors are used. Theorem A,
Proposition B1, Theorem B and Proposition C are unconditional; only the boxed
statement is open.
